Lesson 01 · Phase 0: Reframe

Studying Isn't Learning

From Dr. Justin Sung · "13 Years of No BS Study Advice"

Most people confuse the activity with the outcome. Studying is what you do — reading, highlighting, copying notes. Learning is what sticks — can you remember it, understand it deeply, and use it?

Three hours rewriting notes beautifully feels productive. But if you can't recall them tomorrow without looking, you studied — you didn't learn.

The one-question audit

After any study session, ask: "If I were examined on this right now, how would I do?" If the honest answer is "terribly," the session produced studying — not learning. That gap is your next step: close it with retrieval practice, not more rereading.

The illusion of learning

Worse than not learning is thinking you learned. When you reread, the text feels familiar — "oh yeah, I knew that." But familiarity isn't recall. Clicking "show answer" on a flashcard and recognising it proves you didn't know it. Recognition masquerades as recall, and productive procrastination (rewriting beautiful notes) feels like work but builds almost nothing.

This applies beyond exams. You watch a tutorial on a new CSS framework, follow along, nod at every step. The next morning you open the editor — and blank. You were studying the framework. You hadn't learned it yet.

Which of these produces the most real learning?
You read a chapter, understand every word, and feel confident. Next day you blank. What happened?

Recap: Studying is the activity; learning is the outcome — and it only counts if you can remember, understand, and apply. The feeling of "I get it" is not proof.

Your win: You can now audit any study session with one question — "If examined right now, how would I do?" — and tell productive procrastination from real learning.
