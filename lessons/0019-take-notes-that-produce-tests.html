Lesson 19 · Phase 4: Encoding

Take Notes That Produce Tests

From Dr. Justin Sung · "Learn to Learn" (Full Course)

Notes are not the deliverable — tests are. The note that is never turned into a question you can answer is storage, not learning. This lesson gives you one pipeline: take notes so that every note is a future test, then run the tests.

Why transcription fails

The more faithfully you copy, the less you process. Verbatim capture (typing, or photographing the board) moves words from one surface to another while your brain stays off — that's why laptop note-takers transcribe more and understand less (Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014; the replication story is mixed on laptop-vs-longhand specifically, but the mechanism survives: verbatim capture reduces processing). The learning happens in the decisions you make while writing — what to keep, what to cut, how to connect. No decisions, no learning.

The pipeline in one line

Notes → questions → spaced tests → answers you can produce with the notes closed. Every step is optional except the last two; if you skip to "produce from memory," the notes barely matter.

The ICAP ladder — what your notes force you to do

LevelWhat you do with the materialLearning payoff
I InteractiveYou transform it and dialogue with it (defend, critique, connect to what you know).Highest — the notes become a map you argue with.
C ConstructiveYou produce something new (your own paraphrase, a diagram, an analogy).High — the writing is the processing.
A ActiveYou do something (underline, copy, highlight) without producing new structure.Low — motion without transformation.
P PassiveYou receive (read, listen, watch).Near zero alone.

The test for your own notes: are they the output of constructive (or interactive) work, or a transcript? A transcript is Passive/Active. A page where you decided structure, cut what didn't matter, and connected ideas is Constructive — it already did most of the learning.

The "will this become a test?" rule

Before writing anything, ask: "Can I turn this note into a question I'll fail if I don't understand?" If the answer is no, don't write it. Every note in your system should be answerable-from-memory within a few weeks — otherwise it's clutter that hides the real gaps.
