Keep the Generation on Your Side

AI and learning — the one rule that decides harm or help

Since ChatGPT, the learning question changed shape but not substance: who does the retrieval? The evidence is a perfect paired experiment — the same technology, two opposite designs, opposite outcomes.

The two RCTs that bracket the problem

StudyAI designResult
Bastani et al. (2024 NBER / 2025 PNAS)Unguarded — the AI answers practice problemsPractice scores went up, real-exam learning went down — and students didn't notice.
Kestin et al. (2025, Scientific Reports)Guardrailed tutor — the AI asks, prompts retrieval, withholds answers~Double the learning gains of in-class active learning, in less time.

The generation rule

Generation is learning. Receiving is not. An AI that generates the answer does the learning for you; an AI that makes you generate does the learning with you. Same tool, opposite design — the difference is who produces.

Why the harm is invisible

An answer machine feels great because it creates the fluency illusion on steroids: the polished explanation makes the material feel easy, so your calibration says "got it" while your retrieval ability stays flat. The practice scores rise (the AI did the work) and the exam collapses (you never did). The harm is sneaky precisely because the experience is pleasant.

Operating rules

  • Try first. Attempt the problem from memory before letting AI near it. The attempt is the retrieval.
  • Ask it to test, not to tell. "Quiz me, poke holes, tell me what I got wrong" — not "explain this."
  • Never paste-then-absorb. Reading a generated explanation is the same trap as rereading a textbook: fluency without retrieval.
  • Use it for what it's good at — generating practice questions, varying examples, checking your answers, and acting as an explainer after you've tried.
