Lesson 12 · Phase 3: Retrieval

The 2-Minute Brain Dump

From Dr. Justin Sung · "13 Years of No BS Study Advice"

Recognition hides gaps. Free recall finds them. The cheapest free-recall practice is the 2-minute brain dump: after a study session — or before one — close your notes and write everything you can remember about the topic from memory. No cues, no textbook, no peeking.

Why it works

Free recall (generative) is the strongest retrieval method — you generate and manipulate the knowledge with no cues. And retrieval isn't just a test; it re-encodes — pulling stored memory out and using it strengthens it. The dump is a cycle, not an endpoint.

How to dump

  1. Close everything. Blank page, pen (or blank editor).
  2. Write for 2 minutes. Everything you remember — terms, connections, examples, questions. Unfiltered.
  3. Mark the gaps. What did you miss? What came out wrong? Each gap is a re-encoding target.
  4. Re-encode the gaps. Go back to the source only for what you missed — that's the study part.

Test yourself earlier and more often

Don't save practice tests for the week before the exam — by then you find gaps with no time to fill them. The reference's cadence: every Saturday, test the week; the last Saturday of the month, test the whole month. The dump is the cheap everyday version of that same principle.
