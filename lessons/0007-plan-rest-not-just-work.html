Lesson 07 · Phase 1: Rest & Plan

Plan Rest, Not Just Work

From Dr. Justin Sung · "Productivity is Hard Until You Build Systems Like This"

Most people schedule work blocks and squeeze rest into whatever's left. That's backwards. Rest is not a reward for finishing work — it's a scheduled input that makes the work possible. If you schedule rest first, the work fills the remaining time. If you schedule work first, rest gets squeezed out — and you burn out.

The weekly ritual (rest-first edition)

Spend 30 minutes on Sunday evening setting up your week. Do it in this order:

  1. Block rest anchors first. A weekly nature walk (30 min — the strongest evidence-supported recovery input), daily recovery breaks between sessions (2 / 10 / 30 min), the end-of-day transition (close laptop → shoes on → 15-min walk), and a play container (30–60 min zero-outcome block, content picked from a pre-written menu). Rest anchors are scheduled inputs, not rewards you earn.
  2. Flow match. Schedule deep work (coding, study) in your cognitive peaks (usually morning). Put shallow work (email, admin) in troughs (usually afternoon).
  3. Under-schedule. If you have 8 hours, schedule 6. The buffer absorbs the unexpected — and gives you breathing room. Over-scheduling = always behind, always guilty.
  4. Identify high-risk rest moments — the times you're most likely to skip recovery (e.g., a busy Tuesday afternoon). Script an if-then: "If I finish work late and feel too tired to go outside, I'll at least stand at the window for 2 minutes."

Caution: not all exhaustion is over-planning

Scheduling better rest is a habit fix — but persistent exhaustion isn't always caused by bad habits. If tiredness continues despite better rest, sleep, boundaries, and recovery anchors, it may be burnout, a sleep problem, or another health issue — and recovery may then need professional support, not just a better calendar.

Rest is not what's left — it's what comes first

The toxic productivity trap assumes every second is precious and you should always push harder. Justin Sung's goals include enjoying the journey and not burning out — so exercise, hobbies, and sleep are highly productive, with no guilt. Schedule them first.
