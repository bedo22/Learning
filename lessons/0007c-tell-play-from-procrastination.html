Tell Play from Procrastination

From the Play as Recovery reference · the appraisal channel

You reach for your phone "as a break" — but is it recovery or avoidance? The reference's answer is uncomfortable: the pixels don't change; the appraisal does. Reinecke & Hofmann (2016) tracked 1,094 real media episodes and found the identical behavior counts as recovery when framed as self-care — enjoyment and well-being go up — and as fake play when framed as fleeing work — guilt rises and enjoyment drops.

Wanting vs liking: the wrong and right signal

Berridge & Robinson split motivation in two. Wanting is cue-triggered, dopamine-driven craving — it works without liking. Liking is the actual pleasure of doing. The urge to reach for entertainment is an escape signal, not a need signal. The need for play shows up as liking — enjoyment during the activity. Listen to the second one.

The 5-minute test

You can't predict the need from your desk — it's a system state. So stop predicting: start a play-menu item for 5 minutes. Energizing → keep going, you needed play. Chore-like → your body is asking for sleep or silence. The test uses the reliable measurement (liking), not the unreliable one (wanting).

The guilt tell

Guilt is the tell inside the 5-minute test. Same screen, same game: framed as self-care it recovers; framed as fleeing work it drains. If you feel guilty while playing, you're probably procrastinating — a menu item chosen and framed in advance is harder to guilt.
