Lesson 07e · Phase 1: Rest & Plan

Screen vs Seek

From the Play as Recovery reference · digital play

Is gaming "fake rest"? The reference's answer: the screen is not the problem; motive is. "Active beats passive" needs one refinement from the research — the same pixels are play or procrastination depending on your appraisal, not the medium. Three findings decide whether your screen time recovers you:

1 · Active games are real recovery — mastery is the ingredient

A daily-diary study with 65 employees, 502 measurements (Koçak, Gorgievski & Bakker, 2024) found evening gaming predicts next-morning recovery through mastery; detachment was additive but not decisive. Immersion is what carries that detachment — absorbed, active play (vs passive watching) uncouples you from work (Mella, Iacovides & Cox, CHI 2023).

2 · Passion decides: harmonious vs obsessive

In the same study, harmonious passion — playing because you enjoy it — strengthened recovery; obsessive passion — playing because you must — weakened it. The same wanting/liking split, measured at scale.

3 · Challenge recovers more than ease

Zhao et al. (CHI 2025, N=40 + fNIRS) found intense, high-demand games produced better post-work recovery than easy games — and prefrontal activation actually dropped during intense play. Recovery isn't effortlessness; it's switching control systems into a different, self-chosen challenge.

What makes evening gaming real recovery?
You play because you feel you must. What happens?

Recap: The screen isn't the problem — motive is. Active, immersive play with harmonious passion recovers (mastery is the ingredient); passive watching and obsessive "must" playing don't.

Your win: You can audit one real evening of screen time against the three tests — active or passive? harmonious or obsessive? challenge or ease? — and label it play or fake rest.
