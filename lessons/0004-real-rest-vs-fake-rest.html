Lesson 04 · Phase 1: Rest & Plan

Real Rest vs Fake Rest

From Dr. Justin Sung · "How to Rest So Well You Never Feel Exhausted Again"

You finish a 90-minute focus session, feel drained, and reach for your phone. Ten minutes of scrolling later, you feel worse. What went wrong? You rested — but you didn't recover.

Rest ≠ Recovery

Rest is any break. Recovery requires relaxation — low activity for the system you're trying to recover. After heavy cognitive work (coding, studying, decisions), you need low cognitive activity. Scrolling social media is low-effort but still demands attention and cognition — so it's a broken rest. It doesn't recover you; it steals the time you could have used for real recovery, and leaves you more tired.

Not every entertainment is fake rest, though. The test isn't the medium — it's whether the activity hits at least one recovery dimension (relaxation, detachment, mastery, control). A video you chose and can stop easily can be real rest; the auto-scroll feed that hijacks your attention is what isn't.

The four dimensions of recovery

Research (Sonnentag & Fritz) identifies four things that actually produce recovery:

  1. Relaxation — low activity for the system being recovered.
  2. Psychological detachment — mentally switching off from work; not checking messages "just this once."
  3. Mastery — learning something new with a sense of progress (gym, dance, a language). Recovery only when it's chosen, not when it becomes another obligation or a tracked scoreboard.
  4. Control — spending time the way you choose.

If your break doesn't hit at least one of these, it's probably broken rest. The closest thing to a recovery booster? The evidence suggests nature — soft fascination appears to renew attention, energy, and working-memory capacity.

Why the feed is broken rest

Scrolling demands real attention and cognition — so it never turns off the system you're trying to recover. The tell is guilt: the same 10 minutes counts as recovery when framed as self-care and as draining when framed as fleeing work. The deeper mechanism — the feed as a wanting machine that delivers craving without liking — is unpacked in Lesson 07c and real play as recovery.
