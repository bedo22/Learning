Judge Your Play: Is It Play or Not?

From the Play as Recovery reference · the strict list

The reference doc ends with a scored strict list that judges 27 common indoor, solo, and screen activities against the play definition and the recovery science — each one rated 0–10 on both axes and ranked by the combined score. This lesson gives you the 30-second judge behind that list — so you can verdict any activity, including ones not on the list, in the moment.

Play is a state, not an activity

Stuart Brown's definition: "an ancient, voluntary, inherently pleasurable, apparently purposeless activity… undertaken for its own sake." Note the last clause — the same title (chess, a game, a novel) is play in one session and work in another, depending on one thing: whether outcomes matter. Ranked chess where you chase a rating is closer to work; the identical game with nothing riding on it is play.

The three tests = the definition, compressed

Run all three. All must pass.

  • Chosen? Voluntary — you'd do it even if no one watched and nothing came of it. The moment it's forced (a streak, a "should," a deadline), it stops being play.
  • Liking during? You enjoy it while doing it — not just the urge to start, not the relief of having done it. The urge (wanting) is the wrong signal; liking is the right one.
  • Zero-stakes? The outcome does not matter — no rank, rating, score, completion, or demanded takeaway. If a result rides on it, the outcome-tracking system is on, and play's whole job was to switch it off.

These three are Brown's definition compressed: Chosen = property #2 (voluntary), Liking = the "inherently pleasurable" clause + property #3 (inherent attraction), Zero-stakes = property #1 ("apparently purposeless"). Properties #4–#7 (losing track of time, dropping self-consciousness, improvising, wanting to continue) are confirming markers when present.

What fails — and the three levers that flip it

The activities that fail do so in two classic ways: a wanting-feed (infinite, cue-triggered, no natural end — doomscrolling, autoplay binges, gacha) fails Chosen and Liking; a task in disguise (streaks, ranks, "must learn something from this") fails Zero-stakes. Both are fixable:

  • Remove the stakes — unranked, no streak, no completion. Same activity, different state.
  • Remove the feed — finite container, no autoplay, an external stop. The wanting-machine has no built-in end, so the end must be yours.
  • Judge during, not after — run the 5-minute liking test. Still bored after a genuine attempt? It wasn't the right channel; retriage.

The scoring behind the ranked list

In the reference's strict list every verdict is a number, not just a mark. Play score (0–10) = the weakest of the three tests — min(V·L·Z) — because all three must pass (a chain is as strong as its weakest link: one failing test sinks the definition). Recovery score (0–10) = the mean of the four recovery-experience dimensions — mean(D·R·M·C): detachment, relaxation, mastery, control (the Recovery Experience Questionnaire, Sonnentag & Fritz 2007, rated 0–10 in the style of behavioral-activation activity ratings, Jacobson et al. 1996). The combined rank = Play × Recovery ÷ 10 — play-as-recovery needs both axes, which is why a pure-rest activity (nature) or a mastery activity (ranked play) ranks low here even though each is excellent recovery for its own state. The numbers are priors for how each activity typically goes: for your own session, rate V·L·Z·D·R·M·C on what you just did, and let the 5-minute liking test override the table.

Two questions, not one

The reference is titled Play as Recovery — so for any activity, ask twice: is it play? (the three tests above) and does it recover? (does it offload the system work actually loaded — or just reload it?). The answers don't always agree, and the splits are the interesting cases:

  • Both — zero-stakes engagement on a different channel than work (most play-menu items).
  • Play only — passes the definition but leaves the loaded system loaded: same-channel play (silly coding for a working coder).
  • Recovery only — not play (stakes present) yet still restores a depleted system through mastery, rest, or learning: ranked play under harmonious passion, virtual nature, gamified study.
  • Neither — wanting-feeds and tasks in disguise: fail the definition and load rather than offload.
