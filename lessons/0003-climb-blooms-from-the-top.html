Lesson 03 · Phase 0: Reframe

Climb Bloom's From the Top

From Dr. Justin Sung · "6 Levels of Thinking Every Student MUST Master"

Every piece of studying happens at a level of thinking. There are six, from lowest to highest:

#Thinking → Result
L1Remember → Regurgitate (rereading, flashcards)
L2Understand → Explain (comprehend what it means)
L3Apply → Solve (use one concept to solve a problem)
L4Analyze → Compare (see info in relation to other info)
L5Evaluate → Prioritize ("so what? why does it matter?")
L6Create → Generate (design something new — advanced)

The counterintuitive move

Most people climb bottom-up: remember first, then understand, then apply, then analyze… That's too slow — you forget as you go. The forgetting curve means you spend most of your time relearning.

Instead, start at level 5 (evaluate) and work down. Ask "so what? why does it matter?" — that forces you to analyze and understand in the first place. The brain builds stronger memory at higher levels, so the lower results (regurgitate, explain, solve) arrive as side effects. It does not work in reverse: studying only to regurgitate will never auto-produce comparison or judgment.

The effort is the mechanism

Jumping from level 3 to level 4 makes thinking noticeably harder and slower. Many learners read that as "I'm doing it wrong" and drop back to easy levels — so they never improve. Justin Sung calls this the misinterpreted effort hypothesis. The effort isn't a bug; it's what builds deeper knowledge and stronger memory.

Most people study bottom-up: remember first, then climb. What's the better approach?
You jump from level 3 to level 4 and it feels much harder. What does that mean?

Recap: Don't climb Bloom's bottom-up — start at level 5 (evaluate) and work down. The effort that makes higher-level thinking feel slow is the mechanism that builds stronger memory.

Your win: You can name the 6 levels and explain why starting at "evaluate" produces the lower results (remember, explain, solve) as side effects.
