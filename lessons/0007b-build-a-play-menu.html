Lesson 07b · Phase 1: Rest & Plan

Build a Play Menu

From the Play as Recovery reference · containers, menus & triggers

Why does "I'll relax later" never happen? The paradox of play: the moment a gap of free time appears, you fill it with more work — and the tired brain never chooses play, it just picks from what's open. So you don't plan the play; you plan conditions that make play cheap to start.

The container, the menu, the trigger

  1. Schedule the container, not the content. Book a "zero-outcome block" — 30–60 minutes in the evening, 15–20 after a long focus session — whose only rule is no goals, no learning, no stats, no optimization. The slot is fixed; the content is free. Play requires voluntariness, and a goalless slot guarantees it.
  2. Write a play menu when your mind is fresh. 3–4 items that are low-stakes, active, and have no measurable goal (a casual offline game, tinkering with a silly code project, strumming an instrument, shooting hoops). The tired brain doesn't choose — it picks from the pre-approved list.
  3. Attach play to state triggers, not clock times. If-then plans automate action and hold up under cognitive load — exactly the tired-brain moment. "If I've been in deep work for 2+ hours → 15 minutes of play." "If I close my laptop for the day → 45 minutes from the play menu." "If I catch myself snapping at small friction → play."

Play anchors are scheduled inputs, not rewards you earn

The recovery paradox: people recover least exactly when they need it most. So make the menu on Sunday, when your fresh mind can choose — Tuesday's tired brain only executes. You're writing the menu so you never reach the "too-tired-to-choose" cliff.
