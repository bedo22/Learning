The Recovery Break

From Dr. Justin Sung · "How to Rest So Well…" & "If You Have A Bad Memory…"

After 90 minutes of deep focus, your working memory is depleted. You need a break — but the right kind. The wrong break (scrolling, watching a video, replying to messages) is still cognitive work: it keeps your prefrontal cortex engaged and leaves you more tired. The right break lets your cognitive system go quiet.

The break menu

Match the break to the time you have:

LengthWhat to doWhy it works
2 minStand, stretch, look out a window — ideally at trees or sky.Shifts visual focus to distance; brief soft fascination.
10 minWalk outside or around your home. No screens. Physical movement.Physical relaxation + detachment from the work context.
30 minA walk in nature — a park, a tree-lined street, a garden.Full soft fascination: renews attention, energy, and working memory.

The rule: no screens on breaks

If your break involves a screen, it's almost certainly a broken rest: it demands attention. The exception: a mastery activity you're genuinely learning (not consuming). When in doubt, step outside and look at something green.

When you have no energy

Some days even the 2-minute walk is too much. The fix is to prepare the environment in advance and shrink the action until it's impossible to skip: shoes by the door, a water bottle filled, one tab only. Default to a prepared activity and start for two minutes only — the point is the switch, not the dose. Choose something with no performance outcome, so a "failed" attempt still counts.

This connects to the "don't overeat" principle: when your brain feels overloaded — "this doesn't make sense" or "I feel like I'm about to forget this" — stop consuming. Pause. Give your brain space to consolidate what it already has. A 2-minute window stare is often enough.

Recap: The right break lets your cognitive system go quiet — nature, movement, no screens. Match the break to the time: 2 min (window), 10 min (walk), 30 min (nature).

Your win: You have a concrete break menu (2 / 10 / 30 min) so you never have to think about what to do on a break — you just pick the length and do it.
