Lesson 02 · Phase 0: Reframe

Your Memory Is a Leaky Bucket

From Dr. Justin Sung · "Learn to Learn in 109 minutes"

Your memory isn't a bucket that fills — it's a leaky bucket. The question isn't "how much did I put in?" but "how much stays?"

The forgetting curve

In 1885, Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered that memory fades along a predictable curve. Without review, you forget roughly 50% within an hour, 70% within a day, and 90% within a week. The leak is fast at first, then slows — but by the time you cram for an exam, most of what you studied has already drained out. You're not learning — you're relearning.

This applies beyond exams. You binge-watch a CSS framework tutorial on Saturday, follow every step, nod along. By Monday morning, you open the editor — and most of it is gone. You didn't learn it; you just watched it leak.

Two levers flatten the curve

Spaced retrieval quick win
Test yourself after meaningful forgetting, then re-encode. Each review flattens the curve for that item — it takes longer to forget next time. Start with a 1-day → 1-week → 1-month rhythm.
Better encoding the big prize
Flatten the curve from the start. Poor encoding → steep decay → you must review every 2–3 days (flashcard overwhelm). Good encoding → shallow decay → review every 2–3 weeks and it holds.

Encoding takes months to rewire. Retrieval pays off immediately. Raise retrieval first; let encoding climb slowly alongside.
