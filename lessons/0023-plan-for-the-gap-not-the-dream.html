Plan for the Gap, Not the Dream

Why plans die — and the three-part fix that makes them survive contact with reality

You make a plan on Sunday night, full of energy. By Tuesday it's dead. The failure isn't your discipline — it's the shape of the plan. Plans die for two measured reasons: they underestimate time (the planning fallacy) and they leave the when-and-how open (the intention–action gap). This lesson gives you the three-part fix: decompose, trigger, review.

The fix in one line

Decompose until the first step is trivial, attach it to a trigger, and review weekly against reality. Plan → do → review is a loop, not a document.

The three-part fix

  1. Decompose. Break the goal into steps small enough that the first one needs no willpower — "open the PDF and answer 5 questions." If the first step still feels heavy, it's a project wearing a step's clothes.
  2. Trigger. Put the step on a calendar slot anchored to something you already do — "after morning coffee." Write the if-then for the failure point too: "When I hit a hard problem and want to quit, I'll set a 5-minute timer and re-read the question."
  3. Review. Once a week, compare plan vs actual: what took longer? what didn't happen? Then re-plan with that data — the base rate replaces the fantasy.

One metric is enough

You don't need a productivity app. One line per planned block — "planned 60 min, took 90" — builds your personal base rate within weeks. That's most of the fix: once you know your own real numbers, you stop scheduling fantasy days.
