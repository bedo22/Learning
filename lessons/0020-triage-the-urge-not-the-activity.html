Triage the Urge, Not the Activity

The wanting-vs-liking 2×2, applied to your own urges

An urge is not a need. The pull to open the feed, the novel, the game — that's wanting, and wanting fires for cues, not deficits. The bell is not the fire. This lesson gives you the one tool that tells them apart: the 2×2, judged during the activity, not before it.

Wanting and liking are two systems

Wanting is the urge — cue-triggered, runs without your permission, needs no energy and no decision. Liking is the actual pleasure while doing. They separate: dopamine drives wanting; liking runs on its own circuitry. That's why an hour of scrolling can leave you empty and still re-trigger tomorrow — high wanting, thin liking.

The 2×2 — judge during, not before

Liking presentLiking absent
Wanting presentHealthy — the urge points at something you enjoy. Go.Trap — the feed, the streak, the autoplay. High pull, no pleasure.
Wanting absentStart-problem — you enjoy it once started (play menus exist for this).Depleted — rest, not stimulation.

The trap row is where wanting-feeds live: engineered non-satiation, high wanting, thin liking. The start-problem row is why play needs menus, not motivation. The depleted row is why triage comes first: sometimes the correct answer is sleep, not stimulation.

The 5-minute test and the stop signal

Unsure which cell you're in? Try the thing for five minutes and ask: does this feel energizing, or like a chore? Energizing → liking is present, continue. Chore-like → this is the wrong channel; stop and retriage. And watch for the stop signal: liking saturates. The natural end is when the enjoyment drops — not when the app says "one more."

Deny the action, never the urge

Suppression backfires: it rebounds (ironic process), forbidding raises value (reactance), and rigid rules binge (abstinence-violation). The move is triage, not denial — deny the action, accept the urge, and let it peak and pass. Wanting is the same system that pulls you to food, friends, and learning — it can't be excised, only aimed.
