Lesson 09 · Phase 2: Enablers

Important ≠ Urgent

From Dr. Justin Sung · "Learn to Learn" — Part 4

You have a list that never shrinks. The problem isn't that you're slow — it's that your brain feels urgency, not importance. A blinking email feels urgent and gets answered; the skill that would change your career isn't urgent, so it waits. Forever.

The Eisenhower matrix

Sort every task by urgent vs important. Measure importance not by how it feels but by the consequence of not doing it.

ImportantNot important
UrgentDo now — the real firesDelegate / minimize
Not urgentSchedule — health, skills, the quadrant that changes your life. By the time it's urgent, it's too late.Delete — put as much here as possible. Better to rest than do these.

Two traps that keep you busy but stalled

1 · The urgency trap: doing only urgent things, so the important-but-not-urgent (skills, health) never get scheduled — until they become urgent, and it's too late. 2 · Not deleting enough: clinging to low-value tasks out of fear of tiny consequences. Both feel like productivity; both hold you back.

When a task is neither urgent nor important, do the honest thing: delete it or rest. This connects to real rest — filling those slots with needless busywork is fake productivity, not recovery.

Recap: Important = the consequence of not doing it; urgent = how loud it screams. Do now, schedule, delegate, delete. Protect the many-but-not-urgent: schedule it on the calendar, not in hope.

Your win: You've sorted your real task list into the four quadrants and made one delete call — something you kept out of fear, now gone (or rested off).
