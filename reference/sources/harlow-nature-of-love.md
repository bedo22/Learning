## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Harlow's play-deprived rhesus monkeys — social/play deprivation produced severe deficits, establishing that play is a fundamental need.
- play-as-recovery claims: play deprivation damages development; the doc uses this as the "play matters" historical anchor.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Contact comfort is a primary need in primate infants** — Harlow's cloth-vs-wire-mother experiments showed comfort contact drives attachment, not just feeding.
- **Play/social deprivation is developmentally damaging** — Harlow's deprived-rearing monkeys showed profound social deficits; the doc reads this as play's essential role.
- **Play is not a luxury — it is a need** — the doc's framing "play is a primary need" descends from Harlow's deprivation findings.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The nature of love" — title (the cloth-mother studies; the play-deprivation experiments appear in later Harlow work, also cited in the doc's history row)

## Notes / caveats

- The original "Nature of Love" (1958) is about attachment/comfort, not play *per se*; the play-deprivation experiments are Harlow's *later* work (the doc's history row says "Harlow's play-deprived rhesus monkeys" — that's the 1960s Harlow & Harlow work, e.g. "Learning to love" 1966). The 1958 DOI anchors the author, but the *play-deprivation claim* is his subsequent work.
- The doc's history table groups them ("Harry Harlow's play-deprived rhesus monkeys", 1950s–60s); the digest covers the author's contribution.
- **Does NOT support:** that play deprivation applies identically to human adults; Harlow is a primate-developmental anchor, not recovery science.
