## What the doc(s) claim from this source

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **No longhand advantage found** — in a direct replication with improved controls, longhand and laptop note-taking produced equivalent learning outcomes on both factual and conceptual questions.
- **The original finding may have been inflated** — differences in conditions (e.g., note review time, typing speed restrictions) may have contributed to Mueller & Oppenheimer's original result.
- **The medium matters less than the processing** — what you do with notes (review, summarize, test yourself) matters more than how you take them.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "We failed to replicate the longhand advantage observed by Mueller and Oppenheimer." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a direct replication attempt with a larger sample and improved methodology.
- The failure to replicate doesn't prove the original finding is wrong — it may reflect different populations, materials, or conditions.
- **Does NOT support:** that note-taking medium is irrelevant (it may matter in specific conditions), or that the original study was fraudulent (it was a legitimate finding that may not generalize).
