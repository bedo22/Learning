## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Suppressing a thought makes it return** — the ironic-process theory: monitoring for the unwanted thought keeps it active (white-bear effect).
- **Suppression is counterproductive for control** — the doc's denial-backfire line: "don't think about it" fails.

## Notes / caveats

- The suppression effect is robust in-lab; the meta-analytic magnitude (Wang et al. 2020) is modest.
- The doc pairs it with Wang et al.'s meta-analysis; use the meta-analysis for magnitude, Wegner for mechanism.
- **Does NOT support:** that all control fails — ironic effects are specific to suppression under load.
