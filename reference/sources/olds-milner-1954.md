## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- wanting-vs-liking claims: Olds &amp; Milner (1954) — electrical stimulation of certain brain areas is powerfully reinforcing (the origin of the "pleasure center" idea).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Brain stimulation can be powerfully reinforcing** — rats self-stimulate certain brain regions (septal area), the foundational "reward circuit" finding.
- **This opened the brain-reward research program** — later work (Berridge) showed these sites drive *wanting* more than *liking*.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Positive reinforcement produced by electrical stimulation of septal area" — title (the self-stimulation discovery)

## Notes / caveats

- The "pleasure center" popularization overclaimed — Berridge's later work showed these sites are mostly *wanting* circuitry, not pleasure per se. The doc uses the nuance.
- The doc's wanting-vs-liking thesis is precisely the correction to the Olds-Milner "pleasure center" reading.
- **Does NOT support:** that the stimulated sites are "pleasure centers" — the doc's whole point is they're wanting circuitry.
