# Source digest — Groos, 1898

> "The Play of Animals" — the founding practice theory of play: play as
> instinctive practice for later life. The doc's historical anchor for
> "play precedes mastery" (Groos is the first theorist to claim play is
> preparation — the practice theory).

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Groos's practice theory — play is instinctive practice for later life (the earliest "play is preparation" claim).
- play-as-recovery claims: Groos is the historical root of "play precedes mastery" — the doc's answer that play-without-mastery is the rule, not the exception.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play is practice for life** — Groos: animals play to practice skills needed later; play is biologically purposeful (contra the "useless" view).
- **Play precedes mastery by design** — practice play happens *before* the skill is needed/mastered; the doc's "play is mastery minus the outcome" roots here.
- **Groos is the first scientific play theory** — the practice theory opens the modern play-science tradition (the doc's history row #1).

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "the theory of play... that animals play in order to practice their later occupations" — Groos, *The Play of Animals* (the practice theory; doc's history row)

## Notes / caveats

- Groos is 1898 — the practice theory is the *first* scientific account, but later work (Harlow, Pellis, Burghardt) refined/superseded its mechanism. The doc uses it as history, not current mechanism.
- HathiTrust full-view may be US-only and is bot-flaky (404/403 varies); the doc's link is a faithful scan pointer. Internet Archive copy (playofanimals00groo) is the stable verified link.
- **Does NOT support:** recovery science or the recovery-experience taxonomy; Groos is the historical origin, not a modern empirical source.
