## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Severe dopamine depletion does not abolish positive hedonic reactions to sweet taste** — 6-OHDA lesions that wiped out forebrain dopamine (and caused aphagia — the animals stopped eating) left the *positive taste-reactivity* response to sucrose largely intact, contradicting the anhedonia hypothesis.
- **This is how the field separated liking from wanting empirically** — because the animal still "likes" sweet without dopamine, dopamine is read as the wanting/motivation transmitter, not the pleasure (liking) one.
- **The anhedonia hypothesis (Wise) is the specific claim tested and not confirmed** — the then-leading "dopamine = pleasure" template was judged against a direct, behaviorally separated measure and did not survive.

## Notes / caveats

- **Honest sourcing note:** the abstract was not directly retrievable (APA paywalled; not in EuropePMC/Semantic Scholar). DOI/citation/title/year are Crossref-verified. The empirical finding — 6-OHDA dopamine depletion leaves positive sweet-taste reactivity intact — is corroborated within this shelf's verified digests of Berridge's own reviews (berridge-1996, berridge-wanting-liking 1998) and is the canonical collapse result. Treat quotes as title/paraphrase unless the full text is later retrieved.
- The paper's dopamine loss also produced sensorimotor/motivational deficits (aphagia, akinesia) — so the "collapse" was that *hedonic* reactions survived while *initiating/appetitive* behavior collapsed, which is precisely the wanting-liking dissociation.
- **Does NOT support:** that animals had no problems without dopamine — they were profoundly apathetic/aphagic (wanting collapsed); only *liking* (the hedonic reaction) survived. That asymmetry is the whole point.
