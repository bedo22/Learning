## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Laptop use during lectures is associated with lower learning** — but the effect is partly driven by multitasking, not the note-taking medium itself.
- **When multitasking is controlled, the effect shrinks** — the "laptop problem" is mostly an "off-task browsing problem."
- **The note-taking medium itself has a modest effect** — longhand has a small advantage, but it's the off-task behavior that does the real damage.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The negative effect of laptop use was largely attributable to off-task activity rather than the medium of note-taking." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This meta-analysis focuses on laptop use during lectures (not just note-taking), which includes the distraction component.
- The finding aligns with Flanigan et al. (2024) and Urry et al. (2021).
- **Does NOT support:** that laptops are inherently harmful (they're fine when used on-task), or that longhand is always superior (the effect is modest).
