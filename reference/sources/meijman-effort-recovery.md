# Source digest — Meijman & Mulder, 1998

> The effort-recovery model — the theoretical base under the whole recovery
> shelf. Recovery happens when no further demands are placed on the loaded
> system; work load and recovery are coupled.

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: recovery happens when no further demands are made on the loaded system — the effort-recovery model (Meijman & Mulder, 1998).
- play-as-recovery claims: work and recovery are coupled; insufficient recovery shows up in the next work period (the recovery-paradox lineage).
- play-as-recovery claims: "recovery = offloading the system that was loaded" (the strict list's Recovery definition).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Recovery requires the loaded psychophysiological system to be free of further demands** — the core effort-recovery proposition.
- **Load and recovery are coupled across time** — effort at work and unwinding after are connected; recovery deficit carries into next-day work.
- **This model is the base the REQ operationalizes** — Sonnentag & Fritz's four experiences measure the model's recovery process.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "the recuperation process... the load effects... are released when the demands are absent" — effort-recovery model, Meijman & Mulder 1998 (as summarized by Sonnentag & Fritz 2007, p. 204)

## Notes / caveats

- The 1998 chapter has no DOI; the doc cites it by name. I point the verified link at Sonnentag & Fritz 2007 (which both cites and operationalizes the model), so the link is a real, checkable DOI.
- The model is physiological-behavioral; the doc's "offloading" language is a faithful compression.
- **Does NOT support:** a claim about *which* activities recover (that's the REQ + strict-list scoring); the model just says recovery = no further demands on the loaded system.
