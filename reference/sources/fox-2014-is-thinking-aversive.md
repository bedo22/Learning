## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Re-examining Wilson's data found little support for their strong claims** — after closely examining the dataset, the authors conclude there is little support for "disengaged mind is aversive" as a general truth.
- **"Just thinking" was, overall, not clearly aversive** — enjoyment was rated around neutral (≈5 on the pleasant-unpleasant scale); shocked-then-neutral condition.
- **Shockers and non-shockers did not differ in enjoyment of "just thinking"** — so the shock acts do not index general aversion to thinking.
- **Wilson et al.'s findings (not interpretations) are consistent with prior daydreaming research** — the strong "it's aversive" reading is the contested part.

## Notes / caveats

- This is an **opinion/commentary article**, not a new experiment; it re-analyzes the *same* Wilson dataset.
- The doc uses it to keep its own headline honest (variability, not universality) — matching how the "closer read" framing appears in the prose.
- **Does NOT support:** that the shock study is fake or that idleness is pleasant for everyone — it supports "the aversion is real but variable, not universal."
