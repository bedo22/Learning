## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: idleness aversion — people dislike idleness and seek "justifiable busyness" (Hsee et al., 2010) — cited in the doc's "why doing nothing is hard" passage.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **People are averse to pure idleness** — given a reason, people choose busyness even with no payoff.
- **"Justifiable busyness" is the driver** — the need to justify busyness explains why people fill idle time (the doc's "why rest is hard").

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Idleness aversion and the need for justifiable busyness" — title (experiments showing people prefer busyness with justification)

## Notes / caveats

- Lab experiments — the idleness-aversion effect is robust in-lab; real-world magnitude varies.
- The doc uses it to explain why depleted people reach for *any* activity (including non-recovering ones) instead of resting.
- **Does NOT support:** that all busyness is bad — it explains the *pull* to busyness; the doc's strict list is the corrective.
