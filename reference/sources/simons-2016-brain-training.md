# Source digest — Simons, Boot, Charness, Gathercole, Hofstetter, Karpicke & Murphy, 2016

> Do "brain-training" programs work? The definitive review finding no evidence that commercial brain-training games transfer to real-world cognitive abilities.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **No evidence for far transfer** — brain-training games improve performance on the trained tasks, but the benefits do not transfer to broader cognitive abilities (memory, attention, reasoning).
- **Commercial claims are exaggerated** — the marketing of brain-training products far exceeds the scientific evidence.
- **Active control groups are essential** — studies with passive control groups (no activity) show larger effects than studies with active control groups, suggesting the "benefit" is partly just engaging in *any* stimulating activity.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "We find no evidence from the existing literature that playing brain-training games improves broad cognitive abilities." — the key conclusion

## Notes / caveats

- This is a comprehensive review commissioned by the International Mind, Brain, and Education Society.
- The paper does not claim brain training is *harmful* — only that the claimed benefits are not supported by evidence.
- **Does NOT support:** that all cognitive training is useless (specific training can improve specific skills), or that engaging in mentally stimulating activities has no benefit (it does, just not via the "brain-training" mechanism).
