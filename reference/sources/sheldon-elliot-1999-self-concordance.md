## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Self-concordant goals produce sustained effort** — goals that align with personal interests and values ("self-concordant") are pursued with more persistent effort than goals driven by external pressure or introjected motives.
- **Sustained effort leads to need satisfaction** — self-concordant goals produce progress that satisfies basic psychological needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness), which in turn predicts well-being.
- **The chain is: goal → sustained effort → need satisfaction → well-being** — the self-concordance model is a mediational chain where each link matters.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Self-concordant goals — goals that are consistent with one's enduring interests and values — predicted sustained effort, which in turn predicted need satisfaction and well-being." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a longitudinal study (one semester) with college students. The effects are robust but the timescale is relatively short.
- Self-concordance overlaps with but is distinct from SDT's concept of autonomous motivation. Self-concordance focuses on the *content* of goals (are they aligned with values?), while SDT focuses on the *process* (is the motivation autonomous?).
- **Does NOT support:** that all goals must be perfectly self-concordant to be beneficial (goals that are somewhat aligned still produce some benefit), or that external goals are always harmful.
