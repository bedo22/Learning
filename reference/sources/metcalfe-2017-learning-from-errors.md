## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Errors can enhance learning when followed by feedback** — making an error and then receiving the correct answer produces stronger memory than simply studying the correct answer, because the error creates a "surprise" that strengthens the memory trace.
- **The error itself is not the teacher — the feedback is** — errors without feedback can lead to strengthened wrong answers. The combination of error + correction is what produces the benefit.
- **Metacognitive calibration matters** — people who are poorly calibrated (overconfident) benefit most from error-based learning because the error disrupts their false confidence.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Making errors and then receiving feedback can be an effective way to learn, often as effective or more effective than simply studying correct material." — on the error-enhancement effect

> "The error must be followed by feedback for the error to enhance learning; otherwise the error may simply strengthen the wrong response." — critical caveat

## Notes / caveats

- This is a review paper synthesizing decades of research on error-based learning.
- The effect is most robust for factual/procedural knowledge; complex conceptual learning may require additional support.
- **Does NOT support:** that errors alone are beneficial (without feedback), or that making more errors always leads to more learning. The feedback mechanism is essential.
