## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Caillois's taxonomy — agon (competition), alea (chance), mimicry (pretend/role), ilinx (vertigo/transcendence of order).
- play-as-recovery claims: Caillois is the historical root of the "stakes corrupt play" rule — play with real stakes (agon with external prize) stops being play.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play is organized by four categories: agon, alea, mimicry, ilinx** — supported by the book's central taxonomy.
- **Caillois draws a line between play (paidia) and structured games (ludus)** — the doc's free-play-vs-game distinction in its strict list traces here.
- **"Corrupted" play occurs when stakes, money, or real consequence intrude** — Caillois's "corruption of play" marks the boundary the doc's "Zero-stakes" test enforces.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "play is... an occasion of pure waste: waste of time, energy, ingenuity, skill, and often of money" — Intro (Caillois describing play's non-productive character, which is exactly the doc's "purposeless")

> The "corruption of play" concept — when "the rules become rigid, the stakes real" — Caillois, late chapters

## Notes / caveats

- Caillois's "corruption" is often cited as the origin of "stakes corrupt play" but the doc uses it as the historical anchor; the empirical mechanism (e.g., incentive-tracking override of the liking state) is Cisek/Berridge, cited separately.
- Caillois was describing a *continuum* between free play (paidia) and games (ludus), not a moral judgment — the doc's "zero-stakes marks the boundary" reading is a faithful, if sharper, application.
- **Does NOT support:** recovery science or recovery-experience taxonomy; Caillois is a historical/sociological taxonomy, not empirical recovery evidence.
