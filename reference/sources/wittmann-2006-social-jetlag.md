# Source digest — Wittmann, Dinich, Merrow & Roenneberg, 2006

> Social jetlag: chronic misalignment between biological and social clocks —
> sleeping at different times across the week (e.g., late weekends) creates a
> recurring "time-zone shift" without travel.

## Identity

- **Full citation:** Wittmann, M., Dinich, J., Merrow, M., & Roenneberg, T. (2006). Social jetlag: Misalignment of biological and social time. *Chronobiology International*, 23(1-2), 497–509.
- **DOI:** 10.1080/07420520500545979

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- rest-and-recovery claims (failure modes): shifting sleep timing across the week
  ("banking" sleep debt for weekend catch-up) compounds exhaustion rather than
  repairing it — a stable wake time protects recovery.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Social jetlag is widespread and quantifiable** — the mismatch between mid-sleep on
  free days vs workdays accumulates like weekly travel across time zones.
- **It tracks negative outcomes** — greater social jetlag associates with poorer
  wellbeing and, in later work, metabolic health markers.
- **Timing stability matters independently of duration** — irregular timing disrupts
  circadian alignment even when total sleep is similar.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Misalignment of biological and social time" — title framing.

## Notes / caveats

- Correlational construct (self-reported sleep timing); causal direction for health
  outcomes comes from later experimental/longitudinal work (e.g., Depner 2019).
- **Does NOT support:** that one late night is harmful, or that sleep duration is
  irrelevant — the claim is about *chronic timing instability*.
