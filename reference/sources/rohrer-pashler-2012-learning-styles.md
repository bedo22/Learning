## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **No credible evidence for learning-styles matching has emerged since Pashler et al. (2008)** — the follow-up reaffirms that the meshing hypothesis remains unsupported.
- **The burden of proof is on proponents** — after decades of claims, no well-designed study has demonstrated that matching instruction to learning styles improves outcomes.
- **Common misinterpretations of learning-styles research** — many studies cited as support actually test *modality preferences*, not *learning effectiveness*.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The evidence base for learning-styles has not improved since our original review." — the key conclusion

## Notes / caveats

- This is a brief editorial/commentary (2 pages), not a full review. It summarizes the state of evidence as of 2012.
- The core argument is the same as Pashler et al. (2008): the meshing hypothesis is unsupported.
- **Does NOT support:** that learning preferences don't exist (they do), or that multimodal instruction is bad (it often helps for other reasons).
