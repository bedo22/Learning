## Identity

- **Full citation:** Risko, E. F., Anderson, N., Sarwal, A., Engelhardt, M., & Kingstone, A. (2012). Everyday attention: Mind wandering and computer use during lectures. *Computers & Education*, 58(2), 681–690.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Mind wandering is pervasive in lectures:** Students reported mind wandering frequently during lectures, with significant individual variation.
- **Computer use during lectures is associated with mind wandering:** Students who used laptops for non-lecture purposes showed higher rates of mind wandering.
- **Mind wandering negatively predicts performance:** Higher self-reported mind wandering was associated with lower exam performance.
- **Off-task laptop use correlates with off-task cognition:** Laptop use for non-lecture activities was a behavioral marker of disengaged attention.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Mind wandering was significantly negatively correlated with exam performance." — Results

## Notes / caveats

- Self-report measures of mind wandering — potential for demand characteristics and retrospective bias.
- Correlational design — cannot determine whether mind wandering causes poor performance or whether disengaged students both wander and perform poorly.
- The paper is widely cited in the attention/education literature but the methodology has known limitations.
