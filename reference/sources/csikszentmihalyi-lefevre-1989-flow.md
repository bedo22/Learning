## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- rest-and-recovery claims: "Flow (Csikszentmihalyi) means your attention is *fully consumed by one activity* — challenge + absorption ... you can also be *in flow while still loading the same systems* (deep focused coding = flow, yet zero recovery). Detachment uncouples you from the load; flow just absorbs you."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Flow is the experience of being fully absorbed in a challenging, skill-matched activity** — "optimal experience" (flow) is the doc's "attention fully consumed by one activity."
- **Flow is separable from detaching from work** — an absorbing flow activity can engage the very cognitive systems you were trying to rest; the doc's "flow absorbs you regardless of what's loading you" point.
- **"Optimal experience" is not the same as rest** — a high-challenge activity can be deeply engaging without reducing the load (which is why the doc treats flow ≠ detachment).

## Notes / caveats

- The abstract was not directly retrievable in this run (APA paywalled, not in EuropePMC). Identity is Crossref-verified; the flow-in-work-vs-leisure finding is a well-established result of this same paper (flow is more common at work than in passive leisure, contrary to the stereotype). The doc uses it only for the *flow vs detachment* distinction, which the concept supports.
- The flow-as-recovery subtlety rests less on this single paper than on the broader rest/recovery literature; the doc's claim is conceptual.
- **Does NOT support:** that flow is a form of recovery by itself — the doc explicitly separates flow (absorption) from detachment (unloading), and notes coding-flow produces zero recovery.
