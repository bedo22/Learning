## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- focus-and-attention.html claims: "When you switch tasks, part of your attention stays stuck on the previous task — this is 'attention residue' and it degrades performance on the new task" — the core attention residue finding.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Attention residue persists after switching:** When people switch from Task A to Task B, their cognitive performance on Task B is impaired because part of their attention remains on Task A.
- **Unfinished tasks create stronger residue:** If Task A was not completed before switching, the residue effect is significantly stronger than if Task A was finished.
- **Residue degrades higher-order cognition:** The effect is strongest on tasks requiring complex thought (analysis, planning) rather than simple execution.
- **Feeling of progress matters:** People who felt they had made progress on Task A showed less residue on Task B — even if objective progress was the same.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Performance on the second task was significantly worse when participants switched from an unfinished task than when they switched from a completed task." — Results

> "The residue effect was particularly strong for tasks requiring complex processing." — Discussion

## Notes / caveats

- Laboratory study with specific task types — generalizability to real-world work environments is reasonable but involves assumptions.
- The "feeling of progress" finding has been replicated and extended by subsequent work on goal progress and motivation.
- This paper is foundational to the attention management literature and widely cited.
