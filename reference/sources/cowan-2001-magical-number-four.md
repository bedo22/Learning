# Source digest — Cowan, 2001

> The magical number 4 in short-term memory: the modern refinement that reduced Miller's estimate from ~7 to ~4 chunks. The shelf cites this in memory-techniques as the current best estimate of WM capacity.

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- memory-techniques claims: "Cowan (2001) refines to ~4 chunks; Morra, Patella & Muscella (2024) corroborates: ~4 units brief/strategy-free, ~6–7 with time to recode."
- learning-to-learn-glossary claims: "~5–9 items (Ebbinghaus: ~7; Miller 1956); modern lab estimates ~4 chunks (Cowan 2001)."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The true capacity is approximately 4 chunks** — when chunking is controlled for (i.e., items are presented briefly without time to rehearse or reorganize), capacity is about 4 items, not 7.
- **Miller's 7 includes chunking effects** — the classic 7±2 estimate reflects both raw capacity *and* the ability to reorganize items into meaningful chunks. When chunking is prevented, the number drops to ~4.
- **Attention and encoding limit capacity** — the 4-item limit reflects the focus of attention, not a storage buffer; items outside the focus can be in a activated but not directly accessible state.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The most important and widely accepted conclusion of the present paper is that the true capacity of primary memory is about four chunks or objects." — the central claim

> "The number seven reflects an estimate that includes the effects of chunking." — on the discrepancy with Miller

## Notes / caveats

- This paper was published as part of a BBS target article with extensive commentary — it is well-discussed and well-critiqued.
- Morra et al. (2024) later confirmed: ~4 units with brief/strategy-free presentation, ~6–7 with longer presentation allowing recoding — reconciling the two estimates.
- The 4 vs 7 debate depends on methodology: brief presentation ( favors ~4) vs. typical laboratory conditions (favors ~6–7).
- **Does NOT support:** that working memory has exactly 4 slots (it's an estimate, not a precise count), or that 7 is wrong (it's the estimate under different conditions).
