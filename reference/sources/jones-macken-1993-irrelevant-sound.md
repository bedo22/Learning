# Source digest — Jones & Macken, 1993

> The irrelevant sound effect: changing-state sound (speech, music) disrupts serial recall even when ignored, while steady sound does not. The shelf cites this in memory-techniques as evidence about noise and learning.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Changing-state sound disrupts serial recall** — sound that changes over time (speech, music with varying pitch/rhythm) interferes with short-term memory, even when participants are told to ignore it.
- **Steady, unchanging sound does not disrupt** — a constant hum or tone has minimal effect on serial recall.
- **The effect is automatic** — it occurs even when the sound is irrelevant to the task and participants are instructed to ignore it.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Changing-state speech disrupted serial recall, whereas steady-state sound did not." — the core finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a laboratory study using serial recall tasks. The effect has been replicated extensively across different materials and populations.
- The effect is specific to *serial order* memory (remembering items in sequence), not necessarily to all forms of learning.
- **Does NOT support:** that all noise is harmful (steady sound is fine), or that the effect applies equally to all types of learning tasks (it's strongest for serial recall).
