## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Piaget charted play *stages* that track cognitive development (practice/sensorimotor → symbolic → games with rules) — play reflects and exercises the developing mind.
- play-as-recovery claims: Vygotsky saw play as the "zone of proximal development" — pretend play stretches the child beyond current ability, and play is where the child learns self-regulation through rules.
- play-as-recovery claims: both anchor the developmental tradition; the doc's context callout weaves them as the developmental lens.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play develops in stages that track cognition** (Piaget) — practice play → symbolic play → games-with-rules, mirroring cognitive development.
- **Play is the leading edge of development** (Vygotsky) — "in play a child is always above his average age"; play creates the zone of proximal development.
- **Play is where self-regulation is learned** (Vygotsky) — children follow rules in play that they can't follow outside it.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "in play a child always behaves beyond his average age, above his daily behavior" — Vygotsky, ZPD/play discussion (*Mind in Society*, ch. 7)

> Piaget's three play stages: practice play (sensorimotor), symbolic play (pre-operational), games with rules (operational) — *Play, Dreams and Imitation*

## Notes / caveats

- Both are children's-development theories; the doc applies them historically (how the field arrived at play) and as developmental context — not as adult-recovery mechanism.
- The doc's history row says "1962" for Piaget (the English translation) and clusters Vygotsky in the same 1930s–1962 row; the Vygotsky *Mind in Society* English collection is 1978.
- **Does NOT support:** adult recovery science; neither theorist addresses adult recovery or the recovery-experience taxonomy.
