## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- planning-and-execution.html claims: "People systematically underestimate how long tasks take — even when they have past experience showing they underestimate" — the planning fallacy.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **People underestimate task completion times:** Even when participants had completed similar tasks before, they still underestimated how long the current task would take.
- **Past failure doesn't correct the bias:** People who had previously underestimated completion times on a similar task did not adjust their new estimates upward — the planning fallacy persists despite personal feedback.
- **Specific plans vs general optimism:** People make specific plans (I'll finish by 5pm) based on optimistic scenarios, not base rates — the inside-view dominates.
- **Intention matters:** People who strongly intended to finish quickly made even more underestimate — motivation amplifies the bias.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The majority of participants (only 4 out of 37) finished the essay later than they had predicted." — Results

> "Even participants who had experienced a prior failure to complete the task on time showed little improvement in the accuracy of their time predictions." — Discussion

## Notes / caveats

- Sample was university students writing essays — generalizability to complex projects is assumed but not directly tested.
- The study demonstrates the phenomenon but does not test interventions in detail.
- This is one of the most cited papers on the planning fallacy — foundational to the field.
