## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The incompetent overestimate themselves** — people who score in the bottom quartile on tests of humor, grammar, and logic estimate their ability to be above average.
- **The competent underestimate themselves** — top performers slightly underestimate their relative ability.
- **Training improves calibration** — improving skills also improves the ability to recognize one's own skill level, reducing the gap.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "People tend to overrate their abilities, and this effect is largest for those who are least skilled." — the core finding

## Notes / caveats

- The effect is about *relative* self-assessment, not absolute confidence. Everyone can be confident; the issue is accuracy of self-ranking.
- Recent replication work suggests the effect is partly driven by statistical artifacts (regression to the mean), but the core finding — poor performers have poor metacognition — is robust.
- **Does NOT support:** that all confidence is misplaced, or that expertise guarantees accurate self-assessment.
