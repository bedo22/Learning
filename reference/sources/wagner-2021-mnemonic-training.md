## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Memory athletes' skill is trained, not innate** — ordinary people who trained with the method of loci for 6 weeks showed memory performance comparable to world-class memory athletes.
- **Training produces neural efficiency changes** — fMRI showed that after training, memory-related brain regions became more efficiently organized, with reduced activation during recall.
- **The benefits persisted** — trained participants maintained their improved memory performance in a follow-up, suggesting durable changes.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "After just six weeks of mnemonic training, previously untrained participants showed memory performance comparable to world-class memory athletes." — on the training effect

## Notes / caveats

- The study used a small sample size (n=51 total). The effects are striking but should be interpreted with caution.
- The training used the method of loci (memory palace technique), which is a specific mnemonic strategy.
- **Does NOT support:** that all memory training produces the same benefits, or that the method of loci is the only effective strategy.
