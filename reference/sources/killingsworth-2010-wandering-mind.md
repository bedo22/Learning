## What the doc(s) claim from this source

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Mind-wandering predicts unhappiness** — in a large experience-sampling sample, people were less happy when their minds wandered (regardless of activity).
- **A wandering mind is not restful** — the doc's note that "genuine rest" must not tip into rumination/wandering.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "A wandering mind is an unhappy mind" — title (experience-sampling finding)

## Notes / caveats

- Large-N experience sampling, but correlational (mind-wandering and unhappiness co-occur; direction argued).
- The doc uses it as the caveat that quiet time ≠ rest if it wanders into rumination.
- **Does NOT support:** that all quiet time is bad — it's the wandering/rumination case the doc flags.
