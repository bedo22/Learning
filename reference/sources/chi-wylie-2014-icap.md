# Source digest — Chi & Wylie, 2014

> The ICAP framework: learning behaviors rank Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive — what the learner *does* predicts how much they learn, across evidence from multiple studies.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Four engagement modes** — Passive (receiving), Active (manipulating), Constructive (generating new output), Interactive (dialoguing) — each mode predicts measurably different learning outcomes.
- **The hierarchy is supported across domains** — evidence tables cover hands-on activities, concept mapping, peer discussion, and more.
- **Overlapping behaviors are classified by the dominant mode** — e.g., note-taking can be Active (copying) or Constructive (synthesizing in your own words) depending on what is generated.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive" — the core hypothesis, tested against study-by-study comparisons throughout.

## Notes / caveats

- The framework is a synthesis of existing studies rather than a single experiment; mode classification of a given activity involves judgment.
- **Does NOT support:** that passive exposure has zero value, or that interactive modes are feasible for every learner/task at every moment — ICAP describes relative value, not a mandate.
