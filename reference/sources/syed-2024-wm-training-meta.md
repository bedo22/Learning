## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **WM training produces near-transfer effects** — training improves performance on similar WM tasks (SMD ≈ 0.335), but these gains don't transfer to broader cognitive abilities.
- **Far transfer is negligible** — the effect of WM training on academic performance, reasoning, or everyday cognitive function is near zero.
- **The training gains are real but narrow** — people get better at the trained tasks, but this doesn't make them smarter in any general sense.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Working memory training produced reliable near-transfer effects but negligible far-transfer effects." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a second-order meta-analysis (meta-analysis of meta-analyses), which is the highest level of evidence synthesis.
- SMD ≈ 0.335 is a small-to-moderate effect for near-transfer.
- **Does NOT support:** that WM training is useless (it does improve the trained tasks), or that cognitive training can never work (it might in specific domains).
