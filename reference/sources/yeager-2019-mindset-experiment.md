## Key findings (one line each, with providing quote)

- **Mindset interventions work for specific students in specific contexts** — the intervention improved grades for lower-achieving students in schools with supportive peer norms, but had no effect for higher-achieving students or in unsupportive environments.
- **The effect is small but meaningful at scale** — for the right students in the right context, the intervention produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in GPA.
- **Context is everything** — the same intervention that helps in one school has no effect in another, depending on peer norms, school culture, and teacher support.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The growth-mindset intervention improved grades among lower-achieving students in schools with supportive peer norms." — the key finding

> "In schools where peer norms did not support challenge-seeking, the intervention had no effect." — on context dependence

## Notes / caveats

- This is a massive study: 12,490 9th graders across 65 U.S. schools, with a pre-registered design.
- The effect is modest (about 0.1 grade points for lower-achieving students) but practically meaningful at the population level.
- **Does NOT support:** that growth mindset interventions work for everyone, or that mindset is the primary driver of achievement (it's one factor among many).
