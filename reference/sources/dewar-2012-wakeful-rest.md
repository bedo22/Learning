## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- rest-and-recovery claims: "brief quiet time *after* learning — no input, no task, no screens — measurably improves memory of what you just studied, compared with switching straight into another activity ... (Dewar et al., 2012; systematic review + meta: Weng et al., *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*, 2025)."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **10 minutes of wakeful rest after learning boosts long-term memory** — a brief rest after a story produced significant memory enhancement at 15–30 min and after 7 days.
- **The rest benefit can persist a week even without interim retrieval** — Experiment 2 showed the 7-day boost even when no retrievals occurred in between.
- **The immediate post-learning activity shapes what you remember** — the cognitive activity right after learning matters for how much prose is recalled days later.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "In Experiment 1, wakeful resting led to significant enhancement of memory after a 15- to 30-min period and also after 7 days. In experiment 2, this striking enhancement of memory 7 days after learning was demonstrated even when no retrievals were imposed in the interim" — abstract

## Notes / caveats

- Exactly the mechanism behind the doc's "10–15 min of quiet right after a study session is a cheap consolidation booster."
- The doc pairs this single study with the Weng 2025 meta-analysis, which quantifies the overall effect.
- **Does NOT support:** that wakeful rest replaces sleep consolidation — the doc keeps them separate ("sleep does the heavier consolidation work").
