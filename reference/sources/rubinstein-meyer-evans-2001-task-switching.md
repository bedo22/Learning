## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Task switching has measurable costs** — switching between tasks takes time and produces errors, even when the tasks are simple. The cost increases with task complexity.
- **The cost is in the executive control process** — switching requires disengaging from one task set and engaging another, which is a controlled process that takes time.
- **Switch costs are asymmetric** — switching from a harder task to an easier task costs more than the reverse, suggesting the executive control process is not a simple toggle.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Task switching involves executive control processes that require time and produce errors." — the core finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a laboratory study with simple tasks. Real-world "multitasking" involves more complex switching patterns.
- The study does not claim that *all* forms of divided attention are impossible — only that switching has measurable costs.
- **Does NOT support:** that you can never do two things at once (some parallel processing is possible), or that task-switching costs are always large (they vary with task complexity and familiarity).
