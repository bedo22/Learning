# Source digest — Parten, 1932

> The foundational study of children's *social* play categories (solitary,
> parallel, associative, cooperative). Used by play-as-recovery to note that
> solo play is a legitimate play form, and to position play's social dimension.

## Identity

- **Full citation:** Parten, M. B. (1932). Social participation among pre-school children. *Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology*, 27(3), 243–269.
- **DOI:** 10.1037/h0074524
- **Verified link:** https://doi.org/10.1037/h0074524
- **Open-access link:** none (APA paywalled)
- **First verified:** 2026-08-19
- **Last checked:** 2026-08-19

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Parten's ladder of social play — solitary, parallel, associative, cooperative — is the classic taxonomy of children's play categories.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Parten classified social participation into ascending stages** (solitary → parallel → associative → cooperative) — supported by the paper's title and 1932 quantitative child-observation study.
- **Solo play is a real, early play stage, not a deficient one** — the doc uses the ladder to say solo play is legitimate; solitary play is the first rung, not an absence of play.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Social participation among pre-school children" — title (the study's categories became known as the "Parten stages")

## Notes / caveats

- Parten's research was with children; the doc uses the *rung structure* analogically for adults — the categories transfer, but the age-linked "stages" claim does not.
- Modern play research has questioned whether the stages are strictly hierarchical (contemporaneous later work); the doc uses them as a taxonomy of *modes*, not a strict developmental order.
- **Does NOT support:** adult play, recovery science, or any claim that cooperative play is "better" for recovery than solitary play — that's an empirical recovery question the doc answers elsewhere.
