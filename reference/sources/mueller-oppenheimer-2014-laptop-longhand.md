# Source digest — Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014

> The pen is mightier than the keyboard: longhand versus laptop note-taking. The famous study suggesting longhand notes are better than laptop notes for conceptual questions.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Longhand note-takers performed better on conceptual questions** — students who took notes by hand scored higher on questions requiring understanding of ideas, even though they wrote fewer words.
- **Laptop note-takers wrote more but learned less** — the speed of typing led to more verbatim transcription, which was associated with shallower processing.
- **The medium affects the processing** — longhand forces summarization and paraphrasing (deeper processing), while laptop encourages verbatim recording (shallower processing).

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Participants who took notes on laptops performed significantly worse on conceptual questions than those who took notes longhand." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- Urry et al. (2021) later failed to replicate the longhand advantage in a direct replication: the two media produced equivalent learning. The original finding may have been inflated by the specific conditions.
- The effect is specific to *conceptual* questions — for factual recall, the medium matters less.
- **Does NOT support:** that laptops should be banned, or that longhand is always better. The evidence is mixed and context-dependent.
