## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Meaningful speech disrupts more than meaningless speech** — semantic content (actual words) interferes more with complex language processing than phonological content (nonsense syllables).
- **Children are most vulnerable** — the irrelevant-speech effect is larger in children and young adults than in older adults.
- **Semantic interference is stronger than phonological** — the mechanism involves both phonological loop disruption and semantic processing competition.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Semantic interference was stronger than phonological interference in complex language tasks." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a 2026 meta-analysis from a Chinese-language journal. The DOI may need verification.
- The finding adds nuance to the irrelevant-sound literature by distinguishing semantic vs phonological mechanisms.
- **Does NOT support:** that all speech is equally disruptive (meaningful speech is worse), or that the effect is uniform across ages (children are more affected).
