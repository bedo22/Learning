## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Retrieval practice benefits students across all ages and subjects** — the review synthesized 50 classroom experiments and found consistent benefits from retrieval practice across elementary through university levels, across STEM and humanities.
- **The benefits are durable** — advantages persisted on delayed tests (days to weeks later), not just immediately after the activity.
- **The benefits are robust across implementation formats** — quizzing, free recall, practice testing, and flashcards all produced benefits.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Retrieval practice consistently benefits student learning, with positive effects observed across a wide range of classroom settings." — systematic conclusion

## Notes / caveats

- This is a systematic review, not a meta-analysis (it focuses on applied classroom studies).
- The 50 studies reviewed are specifically from *classroom* settings, not just laboratory studies.
- **Does NOT support:** that retrieval practice works for every student in every context (some studies found no effect for struggling students or under specific conditions).
