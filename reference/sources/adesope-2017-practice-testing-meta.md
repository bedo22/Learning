## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Practice testing produces a large overall effect** — across 217 studies, the weighted mean effect size was d = 0.65, a moderate-to-large effect.
- **The effect holds across ages and subjects** — practice testing benefits students from elementary through university, across STEM and humanities.
- **Feedback enhances but is not required** — practice testing with feedback produces larger effects than testing without feedback, but both outperform restudying.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Practice testing had a moderate to large effect on learning outcomes (d = 0.65)." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is the largest meta-analysis of the testing effect, synthesizing 217 studies.
- The effect size is somewhat smaller than Donoghue & Hattie (2021)'s d = 0.74, likely due to different inclusion criteria.
- **Does NOT support:** that testing is always better than studying (some forms of study can be equally effective), or that the effect is uniform across all conditions.
