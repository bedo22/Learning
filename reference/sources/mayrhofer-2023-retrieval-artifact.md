## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The retrieval > concept-mapping advantage disappears with equated study time** — when both groups get the same total study time, concept mapping and retrieval practice produce equivalent learning.
- **Karpicke & Blunt (2011) used unequal study time** — the original study gave retrieval practice group less study time but still found an advantage, but when time is equated, the gap closes.
- **Both strategies are effective** — the key is active engagement, not the specific strategy.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "When total study time was equated, retrieval practice and concept mapping produced equivalent learning outcomes." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a replication/critique of Karpicke & Blunt (2011). It doesn't否定 retrieval practice — it refines the comparison.
- The finding suggests that the *time* advantage of retrieval practice (it's faster) may be more important than any inherent superiority.
- **Does NOT support:** that retrieval practice is ineffective (it clearly works), or that concept mapping is equally good in all conditions (it depends on time and implementation).
