## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- skill-acquisition claims: "feedback is among the most powerful influences on achievement, but its effects vary enormously by type (Hattie & Timperley, 2007)". The doc's Awareness-stage guidance — ask for the direction of correction, not a grade or judgment — is this paper's four-level model (task → process → self-regulation → self) applied.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Feedback answers three questions** — Where am I going? How am I going? Where to next? Feedback that addresses these is effective; feedback that doesn't is noise.
- **Four levels of feedback** — task, process, self-regulation, and self (praise). Task/process/self-regulation levels are powerful; self-level praise is least effective.
- **Averages mislead** — feedback's average effect size (~0.79 across ~8,000 studies in Hattie's synthesis) hides enormous variance: some forms help a lot, some actively hurt.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The most effective answers to these three questions" — the framing of the whole model (Section: "A Model of Feedback").

## Notes / caveats

- Narrative review with effect-size synthesis, not an RCT; specific d-values vary by study quality.
- **Does NOT support:** that any feedback is better than none — some feedback types have negative effects; nor that grades (self-comparison info) constitute useful feedback.
