## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Rest-related negative affect (RRNA) is a real phenomenon that deserves study outside mind-wandering** — it has been studied mostly through mind-wandering but its neural/psychological determinants differ.
- **RRNA relates to internally directed attention and vigilance** — the propensity depends on how attention is turned inward, and the neural networks implicated are the default-mode, executive, and salience networks.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Rest related negative affect (RRNA) has gained scientific interest in the past decade. However, it is mostly studied within the context of mind-wandering (MW), and the relevance of other psychological and neural aspects of the resting state to its' occurrence has never been studied" — abstract

## Notes / caveats

- The doc sources the *vigilance/brain-turns-inward* framing to this line; note the doc's "while still sharp (high vigilance)" is the doc's synthesis of the abstract's vigilance emphasis.
- This is one specific research program on RRNA; the broader "rest feels negative" point also comes from mind-wandering mood work (Killingsworth & Gilbert).
- **Does NOT support:** that doing nothing is always negative — RRNA is conditional (variability box in the doc); the same resting state can be restorative.
