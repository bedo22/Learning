## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Sleep is a dedicated brain-state for memory consolidation** — repeated replay of hippocampal firing patterns, during slow-wave sleep, drives the transformation of episodic memory into more durable, schema-like neocortical memory.
- **SWS and REM do complementary work** — SWS (with ripples, spindles, slow oscillations) supports the hippocampus→neocortex transfer; REM following SWS balances synaptic rescaling with a homeostatic renormalization process.
- **Sleep consolidation is stronger than wake consolidation** — beyond greater efficacy, sleep differs from waking rest in that spontaneous hippocampal replay *supports* (rather than being impaired by) the neocortical gating.
- **The mechanism is replay-driven** — same basic mechanism as waking rest (replay), but sleep enables it more fully.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "memory replay occurs during slow-wave sleep (SWS) in hippocampal assemblies together with ripples, thalamic spindles, neocortical slow oscillations, and noradrenergic activity. Here, hippocampal replay likely favors the transformation of hippocampus-dependent episodic memory into schema-like neocortical memory" — abstract
> "beyond its greater efficacy, sleep consolidation differs from wake consolidation mainly in that it is supported ... by spontaneous hippocampal replay activity" — abstract
