## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play is a spectrum, not one rigid definition** — supported by: "Rather than holding to one set of criteria... conceptualize play as unfolding along a spectrum."
- **Brown's 7 properties are graded, not a checklist** — "different types of play will embody the characteristics to different extents."
- **Iteration is NOT a necessary feature** — "iteration alone is not a necessary feature for play."
- **Joy IS required** — "Joy is inherent, and required, for an activity to be considered play."
- **Weisberg's 4 criteria are the distilled core** (no purpose/survival link; oftentimes exaggerated; joyful + voluntary; child-led not adult-directed) — quoted within this paper.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Rather than holding to one set of criteria, it might be better to conceptualize play as unfolding along a spectrum, or continuum, that ranges from free play ... to forms of play that are none-the-less child directed but that have inherent goals like guided play, and games." — Discussion of definitions, ¶2

> "different types of play will embody the characteristics to different extents, which will then lead to different benefits for learning and other outcomes." — Playful-learning characteristics section

> "iteration alone is not a necessary feature for play. Yet, play often inspires iteration." — Play inspires iteration section

> "Joy is inherent, and required, for an activity to be considered play." — Joy section

> "Play is often defined as activity done for its own sake, characterized by means rather than ends (the process is more important than any end point or goal), flexibility ..., and positive affect." — opening ¶ (quoting Smith & Pellegrini 2013 framing)

## Notes / caveats

- The paper's context is *children's learning through play* — the doc extends it to adult recovery, which is a legitimate but distinct application.
- Zosh list Brown's 7 properties verbatim (Stuart Brown, 2010 edition) — the doc's 7-property list traces here.
- Weisberg et al. 2013's four distilled criteria are quoted within this paper (no specific purpose/survival link; oftentimes exaggerated; joyful + voluntary; child-led not adult-directed).
- **Does NOT support:** adult recovery specifically; clinical dosing of play; empirical claims about play "duration" — these come from Sonnentag/Burghardt elsewhere.
