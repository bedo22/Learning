## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Positive fantasies alone reduce effort** — purely positive thinking about the future (indulging) actually *decreases* energy and persistence because the mind partially experiences the desired outcome as already achieved.
- **Mental contrasting (MC) energizes pursuit** — contrasting a positive future with a present obstacle creates the *necessity* to act, energizing effort toward the goal.
- **MC + Implementation Intentions (WOOP) is the strongest form** — when mental contrasting is paired with if-then plans ("if obstacle X arises, I will do Y"), the combination produces the most robust behavior change across health, academic, and interpersonal domains.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Indulging in positive fantasies about the future — for example, imagining getting an A on an exam — actually *decreases* energy and effort." — on the counterintuitive finding

> "Mental contrasting links the desired future with the present reality, creating the necessity to bridge the gap." — on the mechanism

## Notes / caveats

- The WOOP framework has been tested in multiple randomized controlled trials across health, education, and interpersonal domains.
- Oettingen's earlier work (WOOP's predecessor, "mental contrasting") was published in *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* (2001) and *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology* (2001).
- The citation in the motivation doc says "Social and Personality Psychology Compass 6:439–453" — this may refer to a different Oettingen 2012 paper; the review paper in *European Review of Social Psychology* is the more comprehensive treatment.
- **Does NOT support:** that positive thinking is always harmful (it's beneficial when combined with obstacle identification), or that WOOP works without genuine commitment to the wish.
