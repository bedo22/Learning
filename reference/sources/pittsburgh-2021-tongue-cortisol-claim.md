## Notes / caveats

- **This digest is a negative-result record — it exists to prevent re-scraping.** Do not cite the Pittsburgh 2021 tongue claim in any doc. If a future primary source surfaces, replace this digest with a standard positive digest and re-verify via Crossref.
- **Misattribution pattern:** The claim conflates several real but distinct literatures: (a) abbreviated progressive muscle relaxation (APMR) where jaw/tongue release is one step among many and *the whole protocol* lowers salivary cortisol in a single small lab study (Pawlow & Jones, 2002, N ≈ 66, one 25-min session, d modest — cannot be reduced to "40 s tongue out"); (b) brief structured breathing from Stanford (Balban et al., 2023, N=108 randomized, 5 min/day × 28 days cyclic sighing vs mindfulness meditation) which improved positive affect and reduced physiological arousal (respiratory rate) — widely popularized via Huberman Lab podcast and often misremembered as "Pittsburgh" or "2021"; (c) yoga Simhasana / lion's breath, which involves tongue protrusion + forceful exhale but has no PubMed-indexed salivary cortisol RCT.
- **Mechanistic plausibility (high-trust physiology, not viral dose):** Jaw/tongue relaxation can plausibly lower tension and facilitate parasympathetic unwinding via reduced muscle afferent activity and via extended exhale breathing that often accompanies the maneuver. High-trust sources on stress physiology (McEwen allostatic load; Meijman Effort-Recovery; Sonnentag recovery experiences) support general relaxation lowering activation, and Kirby-Sands bronchial literature supports slow/extend-exhale breathing modulating autonomic state. Plausibility ≠ evidence for the specific 40-second, tongue-only, cortisol-reduction dose.
- **Search coverage (what was checked):** Crossref works search (queries above, rows 5, filter 2020–2022 for Pittsburgh), PubMed E-utilities esearch + esummary (tongue protrusion cortisol, U Pittsburgh cortisol tongue, PMR salivary cortisol, cyclic sighing cortisol, yoga cortisol simhasana, jaw relaxation cortisol, vagus nerve stimulation salivary cortisol, salivary cortisol breathing relaxation tongue, tongue position stress), and attempted Google Scholar fetch (blocked by bot check). All relevant esearch counts were 0 except PMR (19 hits), VNS salivary cortisol (16 hits), yoga cortisol (240 hits). The 19-hit PMR set and 16-hit VNS set were not systematically title-screened beyond first 3 because none matched the specific 40-s tongue maneuver.
- **Does NOT support:** that 40 s of tongue protrusion alone lowers salivary cortisol; that UPMC or University of Pittsburgh published such a study; that Simhasana/lion's breath has demonstrated cortisol reduction; that vagal tongue maneuvers have demonstrated cortisol reduction; that any YouTube / TikTok demonstration of the maneuver constitutes a citable source.
- **Replication / grading:** Pawlow & Jones (2002) is a single small lab experiment (Biological Psychology, cited 106 times) — not a meta-analysis. Balban (2023) is a single RCT (Cell Reports Medicine, Stanford) — promising for breath, but breath ≠ tongue protrusion. Neither licenses a "40-second fix" claim.
- **Language note:** Arabic queries جامعة بيتسبرج لسان كورتيزول were not separately run (E-utilities English-indexed); the English cross-language search covers the same indexed records. A future Arabic-centric web search could be added if the claim's YouTube URL is provided.
