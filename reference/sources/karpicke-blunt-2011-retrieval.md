## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Retrieval practice beats elaborative studying** — students who practiced retrieving information (free recall) significantly outperformed students who created concept maps, even though concept mapping is one of the most active elaborative strategies.
- **The advantage is robust** — the effect appeared on both factual and complex/science questions, and persisted after a one-week delay.
- **Metacognitive calibration improves** — retrieval practice also helped students more accurately judge what they knew and didn't know, reducing the gap between predicted and actual performance.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Practice testing (retrieval practice) produces substantially better long-term retention than elaborative studying with concept mapping." — abstract

> "Students' predictions of their future performance were significantly more accurate after retrieval practice than after elaborative studying." — on metacognitive calibration

## Notes / caveats

- Concept mapping is a strong elaborative strategy — this paper compares retrieval practice to one of the *best* alternatives, not to rereading.
- The study used science texts (about biology and astronomy), not just word lists.
- Mayrhofer et al. (2023) later suggested the advantage may shrink when study time is matched, but the basic finding remains well-replicated.
- **Does NOT support:** that elaborative strategies are useless, or that concept mapping has no value — it is a good strategy, just not as good as retrieval practice for long-term retention.
