## What the doc(s) claim from this source

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Recovery is the psychophysiological unwinding after effort** — after effort expenditure, the response systems return toward baseline; this unwinding is the recovery process that links day-to-day strain to long-term health.
- **External recovery is needed when internal recovery opportunities are scarce** — long work hours and cognitive/ruminative stress processes sustain physiological activation and block unwinding.
- **Recovery is, at bottom, "no new demands" on the taxed system** — the doc's quote: recovery happens during periods without fresh demands, so the load reactions can settle.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Recovery, a process of psychophysiological unwinding after effort expenditure, is considered a vital link in this relationship" — abstract
> "it is argued that recovery after work (external recovery) is particularly necessitated when recovery opportunities during worktime (internal recovery) are insufficient"

## Notes / caveats

- The specific wording the doc quotes ("Recovery occurs during those periods when no new demands are experienced") is the doc's concise restatement of this effort-recovery position; the paper argues the demands-removal / unwinding mechanism.
- This sits alongside the doc's disputed box, citing Zijlstra et al. 2014 that recovery is ALSO active regulation, not just absence of demand — both can be true.
- **Does NOT support:** that doing nothing alone guarantees recovery once demands stop — the paper's mechanism is unwinding; the doc shades in the "not automatically" caveat elsewhere.
