## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- planning-and-execution.html claims: "People make timid choices (risk-averse) but bold forecasts (overconfident) — the same cognitive architecture that makes us fear losses makes us underestimate task duration" — used to explain the planning fallacy.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Inside-view vs outside-view:** Decision makers use the inside-view (focusing on specifics of the current case) rather than the outside-view (base rates from similar past cases), leading to overconfident forecasts.
- **Planning fallacy is asymmetric:** People are pessimistic about outcomes (fear losses) but optimistic about timing (underestimate duration) — the same system produces both biases.
- **Affective forecasting errors:** People predict how they will feel about outcomes, not how they will actually feel — leading to both risk aversion and overoptimism.
- **Reference points matter:** Outcomes are evaluated relative to a reference point (status quo), not in absolute terms — explaining loss aversion.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The main thesis of this article is that it is easier to make bold forecasts than timid choices." — opening

> "Decision makers tend to focus on the specific features of the current problem and neglect the distribution of outcomes in similar situations." — on the inside-view

## Notes / caveats

- This is a theoretical/review paper synthesizing prospect theory with planning research.
- The inside/outside-view distinction has been enormously influential in decision science and project management.
- The paper does not provide specific debiasing techniques — it identifies the problem, not the solution.
