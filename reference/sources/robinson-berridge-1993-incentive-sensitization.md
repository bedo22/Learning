## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- wanting-vs-liking claims: the original incentive-sensitization theory — drug craving arises from sensitized dopamine systems ("wanting") dissociable from "liking."
- wanting-vs-liking claims: this paper founded the wanting/liking distinction the doc's Pillar 2 uses.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Wanting and liking are dissociable** — the theory's core: incentive salience (wanting) is a distinct process from hedonic impact (liking).
- **Addiction is wanting gone sensitized** — repeated drug use sensitizes the wanting system, producing craving even when liking fades.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "incentive-sensitization theory" — title; the wanting/liking dissociation is the paper's founding claim

## Notes / caveats

- The theory is well-established but the *mechanism* (dopamine = wanting, opioids = liking) is refined by later work (Berridge 1996/2009; Kringelbach & Berridge 2009).
- The doc uses it as the foundational statement; the 1998 and 2016 papers are the elaborated versions.
- **Does NOT support:** that wanting and liking never co-occur — they usually do; the dissociation is the pathological/useful gap.
