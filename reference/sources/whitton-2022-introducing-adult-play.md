## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: adults need permission to play — the book argues that playful approaches can make the world a better place when applied to adult learning.
- play-as-recovery claims: play in adulthood is qualitatively different from childhood play but equally important.
- play-as-recovery claims: one-off playful activities can be starting points for reclaiming play.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play in adulthood is under-studied but important** — the book introduces adult play as a legitimate area of research: "This is a book about play in adulthood and how play can make the world a better place."
- **Playful approaches are increasingly used to engage adults** — in interactive media and education: "playful approaches are used more often to engage adults in interactive media."
- **The book moves from one-off activities to playful redesign** — of whole programmes: "from one-off activities or playful interventions to the playful redesign of whole programmes."

## Key quotes (with locations)

## Notes / caveats

- Book chapter — conceptual framework, not empirical evidence. The book covers play across adult learning contexts.
- The key contribution for the shelf is the conceptual argument: adults can reclaim play, and playful approaches work in adult contexts.
- **Does NOT support:** specific play prescriptions, or that play deprivation causes specific clinical outcomes.
