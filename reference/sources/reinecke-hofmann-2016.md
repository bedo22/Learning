## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: the same media use can be recovery *or* procrastination depending on the appraisal/motive — "slacking off" vs "winding down."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The same behavior can be recovery or procrastination** — the ESM study shows the *motive/appraisal* (recovery vs avoidance) distinguishes them, not the medium.
- **Winding down (recovery) vs slacking off (procrastination) are different experiences of the same activity** — the doc's strict-list "the flip is named" rows use this.
- **Recovery is self-affirming; procrastination is self-defeating** — the distinction lives in the appraisal, matching the doc's Bateson-framing point.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "media use for recovery versus procrastination" — title/abstract (the recovery-vs-procrastination distinction)

## Notes / caveats

- ESM design (experience sampling) — correlational, self-report; the *motive* is measured, not manipulated.
- The doc uses it as the empirical anchor that recovery vs procrastination is appraisal-based — a faithful, direct application.
- **Does NOT support:** that play specifically (Brown-style) is the mechanism; Reinecke & Hofmann study media use broadly, not play-definitional states.
