# Source digest — Kahneman & Tversky, 1979

> Intuitive prediction: biases and corrective procedures — the founding statement of the
> planning fallacy: people underestimate task completion times even when they know,
> from experience, that similar tasks ran late. Book chapter (no publisher DOI).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The planning fallacy** — predictions of task duration are unrealistically close to best-case scenarios and insensitive to known distributional data about similar tasks.
- **Corrective procedure** — rely on the statistics of the reference class ("how did similar tasks go?") rather than the inside details of the current plan.
- **Biases survive sophistication** — awareness of the bias does not eliminate it; structural correction (reference-class forecasting) is required.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "biases and corrective procedures" — title framing: the paper pairs each bias with a debiasing move.

## Notes / caveats

- Conceptual/theoretical chapter with illustrative studies, not a single experiment; the famous empirical demonstration of deadline underestimation is Buehler, Griffin & Ross (1994) — digested separately.
- **Does NOT support:** that all estimates are equally biased, or that optimism is always irrational (the optimistic-pessimistic tradeoffs are discussed in later work).
