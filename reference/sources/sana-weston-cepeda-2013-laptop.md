## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Laptop multitasking harms the user's learning** — students who multitasked on laptops during a lecture scored lower on a subsequent test than students who didn't.
- **Nearby peers are also harmed** — students who could see the multitasking screen *also* scored lower, even though they weren't multitasking themselves. The distraction is contagious.
- **The effect is dose-dependent** — more multitasking = worse performance.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Multitasking on a laptop negatively impacts not only the user's learning but also the learning of peers who can see the screen." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a laboratory study simulating a lecture environment, not a field study in a real classroom.
- The effect size is moderate — laptop multitasking is not catastrophic, but it consistently degrades performance.
- **Does NOT support:** that laptops should be banned from all classrooms (laptops can also be beneficial for note-taking), or that the effect is unique to laptops (any distracting device would produce similar effects).
