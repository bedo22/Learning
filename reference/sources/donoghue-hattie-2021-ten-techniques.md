## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Distributed practice is the most effective technique (d = 0.85)** — spacing study sessions over time produces the largest average effect across 242 studies.
- **Practice testing is second most effective (d = 0.74)** — self-testing and quizzing produce strong benefits, larger than most other techniques.
- **Elaborative interrogation and self-explanation are also effective** — both produce moderate effects (d ≈ 0.5–0.6).
- **Rereading and highlighting are least effective** — despite being the most popular student strategies, they produce minimal benefits.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Distributed practice (d = 0.85) and practice testing (d = 0.74) were the most effective learning techniques." — the ranking

## Notes / caveats

- This is a meta-analysis of 242 studies with 169,179 participants — the largest quantitative synthesis of the ten techniques.
- The effect sizes are averages across many studies; individual effects vary by subject, age, and implementation.
- **Does NOT support:** that any technique is universally best (the optimal technique depends on the material and context), or that low-ranked techniques are useless (they may be useful as supplements).
