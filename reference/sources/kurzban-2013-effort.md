# Source digest — Kurzban et al., 2013

> The opportunity-cost model of subjective effort. The doc's "play as
> opportunity cost / effort avoidance" counterpoint — effort feels costly
> because it uses resources that could be spent elsewhere.

## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: effort is experienced as costly because of its opportunity cost — using resources now forgoes using them elsewhere (Kurzban et al., 2013).
- play-as-recovery cites Kurzban in the "effort is opportunity cost" passage, alongside Kool & Botvinick and Geana.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Subjective effort tracks opportunity cost, not expended energy** — effort feels high when current use precludes more valuable alternatives.
- **This explains why "recovery" feels restorative** — after work, the opportunity cost of play is low, so play doesn't feel effortful; the doc's "play is cheap when you're depleted" note.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "subjective effort... [is] a function of the opportunity cost of current task engagement" — target article abstract

## Notes / caveats

- The opportunity-cost account is one model of effort (contested vs limited-resource accounts; the BBS commentary debate). The doc uses it as a *complementary* lens, not the sole mechanism.
- **Does NOT support:** a claim that effort is *only* opportunity cost, or that recovery is purely economic; it's one model the doc weaves in.
