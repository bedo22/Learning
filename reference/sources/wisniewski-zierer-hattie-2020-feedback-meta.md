## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Feedback type matters enormously:** Task-level and process-level feedback (about the work itself) has much larger effects on learning than person-level feedback (praise about the person).
- **Task/process feedback d ≈ 0.4–0.8:** Feedback directed at the task or process produces medium-to-large effects on achievement.
- **Person-directed praise d ≈ 0.1:** Feedback directed at the person ("you're smart") produces negligible effects on learning.
- **~2,000 effects across 434 studies:** This is the largest meta-analysis of educational feedback to date, providing strong statistical power for the effect estimates.
- **Feedback timing and specificity matter:** More immediate and specific feedback produces larger effects than delayed or general feedback.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Task and process feedback showed the largest effects on achievement (d = 0.41 to 0.77)." — Results

> "Feedback directed at the person showed the smallest effects (d = 0.09)." — Results

## Notes / caveats

- This is a meta-analysis of educational feedback — the findings apply primarily to learning contexts, not workplace feedback generally.
- The large overall effect of feedback (d ≈ 0.6) masks enormous variability — feedback can be harmful if poorly timed or delivered.
- The person/praise finding is consistent with Dweck's growth mindset research but does not directly test it.
