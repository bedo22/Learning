## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- rest-and-recovery claims: "load reactions return to baseline once you stop, and that unwinding *is* recovery (Demerouti et al., 2009 — recovery as psychophysiological unwinding)."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Recovery is psychophysiological unwinding** — the load reactions (HR, cortisol) return to baseline when demands stop; that unwinding is recovery.
- **Daily, not just weekly, dynamics** — the chapter consolidates daily recovery-from-work research.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "recovery as psychophysiological unwinding" — the doc's description; the chapter's thesis is daily recovery of load reactions

## Notes / caveats

- Book chapter in the *Current Perspectives on Job-Stress Recovery* volume — a consolidation piece, not a single experiment.
- The doc cites it for the unwinding mechanism, aligned with Meijman's effort-recovery model.
- **Does NOT support:** play-specific claims; it's the general psychophysiological recovery mechanism.
