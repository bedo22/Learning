## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Panksepp identified a distinct "PLAY system" — a subcortical, evolutionarily old brain circuit that generates playful rough-and-tumble.
- play-as-recovery claims: play is a *brain state* (Panksepp) — the doc's context callout weaves Panksepp as the brain-state lens.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play has its own brain circuitry** — Panksepp's affective-neuroscience mapping of the PLAY system (rough-and-tumble joy) is distinct from other affect systems.
- **Play is a state, not an activity** — the doc's "play is a state" claim traces to Panksepp's systems-level framing.
- **Rough-and-tumble play is the prototypical PLAY-system behavior** — Panksepp & Burgdorf's rat-play studies anchor the system.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "PLAY" — one of the seven primary-process emotional systems (Panksepp 1998, *Affective Neuroscience*)

## Notes / caveats

- The OUP *Affective Neuroscience* DOI is verified (10.1093/oso/9780195096736.001.0001, crossref 200). The Panksepp & Burgdorf "Laughing rats" paper is verified (10.1016/s0031-9384(03)00159-8).
- The doc uses Panksepp as the brain-state lens in the context callout; the empirical recovery science is Sonnentag elsewhere.
- **Does NOT support:** that PLAY-system activity alone equals *recovery*; it's the neural substrate, not the recovery-experience measurement.
