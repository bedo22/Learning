## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Wanting ≠ liking:** Incentive salience (wanting) and hedonic impact (liking) are neurobiologically dissociable systems — dopamine drives wanting, opioid systems drive liking.
- **Dopamine is not the "pleasure chemical":** Dopamine mediates motivational pull toward rewards, not the enjoyment of them.
- **Wanting can exist without liking:** Animals with depleted dopamine still enjoy food when it is placed in their mouth but will not work to obtain it.
- **Liking can exist without wanting:** Animals with opioid antagonists show reduced pleasure reactions but still seek food.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "We propose that the mesolimbic dopamine system mediates incentive salience — 'wanting' of rewards, but not their 'liking' or hedonic impact." — abstract

> "Dopamine depletion eliminates the vigorous instrumental responding for food rewards, but does not reduce the hedonic reactions to food." — main text

## Notes / caveats

- This is a theoretical review paper, not an original experiment — it synthesizes evidence from multiple studies.
- The wanting/liking distinction has been highly influential but is not universally accepted; some researchers argue dopamine does contribute to hedonic processing.
- The paper focuses on food reward in rats; generalization to human complex rewards (money, social approval, information) involves additional assumptions.
