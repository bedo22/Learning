## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Mindset has a weak relationship with academic achievement** — the correlation between growth mindset and GPA is r = 0.10, a small effect that explains only 1% of variance.
- **Mindset interventions have small effects** — interventions that teach growth mindset produce a small average effect (d = 0.08) on academic achievement, and the effect is larger for academically at-risk students.
- **Context matters enormously** — the effect of mindset is moderated by socioeconomic status, prior achievement, and the educational environment.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The relationship between mindset and academic achievement was weak (r = 0.10)." — the key finding

> "Mindset interventions produced a small but statistically significant effect on academic achievement (d = 0.08)." — on interventions

## Notes / caveats

- These are two separate meta-analyses (one on the correlation, one on interventions) published in the same paper.
- The small effect size does not mean mindset is irrelevant — it means it's one factor among many, not the silver bullet it's often marketed as.
- Yeager et al. (2019) later found that the effect is strongly context-dependent, which the meta-analyses' averaging obscures.
- **Does NOT support:** that mindset is completely irrelevant (it has a real but small effect), or that teaching growth mindset is harmful.
