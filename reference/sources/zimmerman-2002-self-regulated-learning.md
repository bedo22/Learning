## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Self-regulation is a cyclical process** — it involves three phases: forethought (goal setting, strategy planning), performance (self-monitoring, strategy use), and self-reflection (self-evaluation, adaptation).
- **Self-regulated learners outperform others** — students who set goals, monitor their progress, and adapt their strategies learn more effectively than those who don't.
- **Self-regulation is learnable** — it is not a fixed trait but a set of skills that can be taught and improved through practice.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Self-regulated learners are described as metacognitively, motivationally, and behaviorally active participants in their own learning process." — the definition

## Notes / caveats

- This is a theoretical overview paper, not an empirical study.
- The three-phase model is one of several self-regulation frameworks (others include Winne & Hadwin, Pintrich).
- **Does NOT support:** that self-regulation is sufficient without domain knowledge, or that all learners benefit equally from self-regulation training.
