## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: adult play and playfulness are ways of experiencing life motivated by positive emotions, humor, and engagement in activities that promote relationships.
- play-as-recovery claims: five themes of adult playfulness — A Positive Outlook, Activities, Relationships, Humor, and Experiencing Life.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Adult playfulness has five core themes** — A Positive Outlook, Activities, Relationships, Humor, and Experiencing Life: "five main themes emerged: A Positive Outlook, Activities, Relationships, Humor, and Experiencing Life."
- **Playfulness is motivated by positive emotions and relationship-building** — "adult play and playfulness are ways of experiencing life that are often motivated by the experience of positive emotions, humor, and engagement in activities that promote relationships."
- **Playfulness relates to positive mental health outcomes** — "provide guideposts for future research on play and playfulness as essential strengths that enable individuals to thrive."
- **837 adults participated** — large qualitative sample: "A total of 837 adult participants (Mage = 23.52, SD = 6.28) provided detailed answers."

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "What does it mean to you to be playful?" — the elicitation question (method)

> "adult play and playfulness are ways of experiencing life that are often motivated by the experience of positive emotions, humor, and engagement in activities that promote relationships and that relate to positive mental health outcomes" — abstract conclusion

## Notes / caveats

- Qualitative study — themes are descriptive, not causal. The sample skews young (M age = 23.52), so the themes may not fully capture play in middle/older age.
- The five themes provide a framework for understanding what adults actually mean when they say they're "being playful" — useful for designing play interventions.
- **Does NOT support:** that any specific activity is play, or that playfulness declines with age (that's Brauer 2024). The study describes what playfulness means to adults, not how much they have.
