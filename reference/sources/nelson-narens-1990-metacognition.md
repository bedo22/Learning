## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Metacognition has two components: monitoring and control** — monitoring tells you what you know; control uses that information to allocate study time and select strategies.
- **Monitoring and control are linked** — inaccurate monitoring leads to poor control decisions (e.g., stopping study too early because you think you know the material).
- **The "feeling of knowing"** — people have a sense of whether they know something even when they can't retrieve it, and this feeling guides future study behavior.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Metacognition consists of two processes: monitoring (the evaluation of one's own cognitive processes) and control (the regulation of those processes)." — the framework

## Notes / caveats

- This is a theoretical framework paper that has become the standard reference for metacognition research.
- The monitoring-control framework explains many study-behavior phenomena: the labor-in-vain effect, the spacing effect, and the testing effect.
- **Does NOT support:** that monitoring is always accurate (it's often miscalibrated), or that control is always effective (it depends on the accuracy of monitoring).
