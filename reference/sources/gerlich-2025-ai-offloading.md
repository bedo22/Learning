## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **AI use ↔ offloading ↔ weaker critical thinking** — survey data (n ≈ 666, UK) shows frequent AI-tool use associated with higher cognitive offloading and lower critical-thinking performance.
- **Age moderates the association** — the negative link is strongest in the 17–25 group.
- **The author frames it as risk, not destiny** — the correlational design cannot establish that AI use causes thinking decline.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Impacts on Cognitive Offloading and the Future of Critical Thinking" — title framing.

## Notes / caveats

- Cross-sectional self-report data; no causal identification; MDPI journal (shelf Tier 3 — supplementary evidence).
- **Does NOT support:** that AI use damages brains or that guarded AI tutoring is harmful — the RCT evidence (Bastani 2025, Kestin 2025) shows harm under unguarded use and gains under retrieval-prompting designs.
