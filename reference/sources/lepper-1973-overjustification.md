## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Extrinsic rewards undermine intrinsic motivation** — children who were promised a "Good Player" reward for drawing (which they already enjoyed) subsequently drew less during free time than children who received no reward.
- **The overjustification effect** — when an external reward is perceived as controlling behavior, people attribute their activity to the reward rather than to their own interest, reducing intrinsic motivation.
- **Expected vs unexpected rewards differ** — unexpected rewards did not undermine intrinsic motivation; only expected, contingent rewards did.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Children who expected an extrinsic reward for drawing spent significantly less time drawing in a free-choice period than children who received no reward." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a classic study with a small sample (n=51 preschoolers). The effect has been replicated extensively but the magnitude varies.
- The effect is most robust for *expected, tangible, task-contingent* rewards. Verbal praise, unexpected rewards, and informational feedback often do not undermine motivation.
- Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory provides the broader theoretical framework for understanding when rewards help vs. harm.
- **Does NOT support:** that all rewards are harmful (unexpected rewards and informational feedback are fine), or that the overjustification effect applies equally to all ages and cultures.
