## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Burghardt's criteria for recognizing play — still the most cited in the field — define play as behavior that is "not fully functional" in the form or context in which it is expressed (criterion 1).
- play-as-recovery claims: criterion 3 — play looks "incomplete (generally through inhibited or dropped final elements), exaggerated, awkward, or precocious."
- play-as-recovery claims: play is by definition the *less-than-masterful* version of an act — play-without-mastery is the definition, not an exception.
- play-as-recovery claims: the five criteria are a graded set for cross-species recognition (also used to test whether non-mammals play, e.g. bumblebees in Galpayage Dona et al., 2022).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play is, by definition, the less-than-masterful version of an act** — supported by Criterion 1: behavior is "not fully functional" in the form or context in which it is expressed.
- **Play looks structurally incomplete/exaggerated/awkward compared to the real act** — supported by Criterion 3 quote below.
- **The 5 criteria are graded, applied cross-species** — supported by: the criteria are designed for recognizing play across species, not as a fitness checklist per behavior.
- **Play-without-mastery is not an exception; it IS the definition** — supported by Criterion 1 ("not fully functional" is the defining feature, not a deficiency).

## Notes / caveats

- The five criteria are: (1) not fully functional, (2) voluntary/spontaneous/pleasurable/rewarding, (3) differs structurally from functional behavior (incomplete/exaggerated/awkward), (4) repeated but not stereotyped, (5) initiated in a relaxed field.
- The doc quotes criteria 1 and 3 in the "play precedes mastery" section; the other three are referenced implicitly ("five criteria for recognizing play").
- Burghardt's criteria are applied *graded* across species — an activity qualifies when the criteria are met; the bumblebee-play study (2022) applied all five as tests.
- **Does NOT support:** human recovery science, the recovery-experience taxonomy, or claims about *which* adult activities recover depleted systems — Burghardt is a cross-species definitional source, not a recovery-mechanism one. Adult-recovery claims come from Sonnentag/Zosh elsewhere.
