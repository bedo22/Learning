## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: the field's distilled core of play definitions is four criteria — (1) no specific purpose nor linked to survival, (2) oftentimes exaggerated in form, (3) joyful and voluntary participation, (4) child-led not adult-directed.
- play-as-recovery claims: for adults, criterion (4) "child-led, not adult-directed" becomes "self-directed, not externally directed."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The field's distilled core of play = exactly 4 criteria** — supported by: the reproduction of Weisberg's 4-criteria list (quoted in Key quotes below).
- **Criterion 4 is a childhood framing** ("child-led, not adult-directed") — the doc's adult adaptation ("self-directed") is an extension, not the paper's word.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> (As quoted in Zosh et al., 2018, Definitions section): "Play (1) has to have no specific purpose nor be linked to survival, (2) can oftentimes be exaggerated — e.g., playful experiences often are not necessarily related to how things work in regular, everyday life, (3) requires both joyful and voluntary participation, and (4) is child-led, not adult-directed."

## Notes / caveats

- The four criteria are quoted *within* Zosh et al. 2018 — the doc cites Weisberg directly but the quote was verified through Zosh's reproduction. Both are primary sources for the criteria list.
- Weisberg's focus is children's guided play in education; the "child-led" criterion is explicitly a childhood framing. The adult adaptation ("self-directed") is the doc's own extension, marked as such.
- **Does NOT support:** adult recovery; a claim that adults' play requires a pedagogical/curricular goal — the paper is about *guided* play in classrooms, distinct from the doc's free-play-as-recovery frame.
