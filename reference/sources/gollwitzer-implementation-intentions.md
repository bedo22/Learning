## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: "Gollwitzer's if-then research: cue-linked plans automate response" — implementation intentions automate starting (the doc's scripted-cue passage).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **If-then plans automate initiation** — implementation intentions link a cue to a response, reducing the need for deliberation at the moment of action.
- **Implementation intentions reliably boost goal achievement** — the 2006 meta-analysis shows a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans" — 1999 title (if-then plans)

## Notes / caveats

- The effect is reliable for *initiating* actions; it does not remove the need for liking/voluntary choice (the doc's play core).
- The doc uses it for starting (the "tired-PFC moment"), not for forcing play — a distinction the doc makes.
- **Does NOT support:** that scripting play preserves play — the doc's "you can't plan play" dark twin is the guardrail.
