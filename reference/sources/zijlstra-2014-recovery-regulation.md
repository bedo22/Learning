## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Recovery is active regulation, not just demand-absence** — the reconceptualization: recovery harmonizes current state with next-task-required state.
- **Passive rest is insufficient as a theory** — resting without regulating may not prepare you for the next demand.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "recovery to regulation" — the reconceptualization in the title: recovery as a regulation process

## Notes / caveats

- A reconceptualization/theory piece — argues a shift in how recovery is studied, not a single experiment.
- The doc cites it honestly as the *counterargument* to pure demand-absence; this is the doc's "doing nothing isn't automatically recovery" note.
- **Does NOT support:** that all passive rest fails — it reframes recovery as regulation; the doc keeps both views (rest can become recovery if genuine).
