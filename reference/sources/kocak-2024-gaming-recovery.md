## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: gaming recovers through mastery experiences (Koçak, daily-diary study, 65 employees, 502 measurements).
- play-as-recovery claims: intense games recover more than easy ones (via the mastery channel).
- play-as-recovery claims: gaming predicts next-morning recovery through mastery (Koçak).
- play-as-recovery claims in the strict list: gaming stays on the mastery-recovery channel, not play-recovery.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Video games recover depleted resources via the mastery experience, not the play experience** — supported by the study's finding that recovery from gaming runs through *mastery*.
- **Intense games recover more than easy ones** — higher game demand → stronger mastery → more recovery (the doc's strict-list note).
- **Gaming's recovery effect carries to next-morning well-being** — daily-diary design (65 employees, 502 measurements) links evening gaming to next-day recovery.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Recovery from work by playing video games" — title (the doc's mastery-channel claim anchors here)

## Notes / caveats

- Observational daily diary — correlational, not causal (recovery and gaming co-vary; direction argued, not proven).
- Sample is employees who chose to game; self-report; no abstinence-control comparison in the headline claim as the doc uses it.
- The doc uses Koçak specifically to separate the *mastery* channel from the *play* channel — a faithful but sharp reading; Koçak reports mastery as the mechanism, not "play-as-recovery."
- **Does NOT support:** that *play* (voluntary/liking/zero-stakes, Brown-style) recovers — Koçak's mechanism is mastery, which is the doc's distinct recovery *channel*.
