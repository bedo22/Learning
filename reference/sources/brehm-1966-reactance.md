## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- wanting-vs-liking claims: "Forbidding raises value. Reactance theory (Brehm, 1966)" — when you forbid yourself something, it becomes more attractive.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Restricting freedom triggers reactance** — when a behavior is forbidden, people experience psychological reactance (motivation to restore the freedom).
- **Forbidden things become more attractive** — the doc's denial-backfire line: "forbidding raises value."

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "reactance" — the motivational state when a freedom is threatened/eliminated (Brehm, 1966; formalized in Brehm & Brehm, 1981)

## Notes / caveats

- The 1966 book has no DOI; the verified link is the 1981 Brehm & Brehm chapter formalizing reactance theory.
- The doc pairs it with Wegner/Wang (suppression) and Marlatt (abstinence violation) in the denial-backfire cluster.
- **Does NOT support:** that all restriction backfires — reactance magnitude depends on freedom importance; the doc uses it for self-imposed forbidding.
