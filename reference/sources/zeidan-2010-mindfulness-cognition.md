## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Brief mindfulness training improves cognition:** Four days of 20-minute mindfulness meditation sessions improved performance on measures of executive attention and cognition.
- **Effects appear after very brief training:** Unlike many meditation studies requiring weeks of practice, significant cognitive benefits appeared after just 80 minutes of total training.
- **Improvements on specific cognitive domains:** Performance improved on tasks measuring sustained attention, visuospatial processing, and executive functioning.
- **Active control group:** The study used an active control (relaxation training), making the effect attributable to mindfulness specifically, not just relaxation.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Relative to the control group, participants who received the mindfulness training demonstrated significantly greater improvements in executive attention and cognition." — Results

## Notes / caveats

- Small sample size (n = 60) — the effect sizes should be interpreted with caution.
- The four-day training period is unusually brief for meditation research — this is both the paper's strength (practical significance) and a limitation (durability unknown).
- Subsequent research has both replicated and questioned these findings; the mindfulness-cognition literature is active and contested.
