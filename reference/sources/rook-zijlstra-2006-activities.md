## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: activity *type* matters for recovery — some low-effort leisure does little or nothing (Rook & Zijlstra, 2006) — the doc's "passive rest has mixed evidence" note.
- rest-and-recovery claims: "Some studies find low-effort leisure does little or nothing for recovery (Rook & Zijlstra, 2006)."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Activity type determines recovery, not just "resting"** — Rook & Zijlstra compared activity types and their recovery contributions.
- **Low-effort leisure can fail to recover** — passive/low-effort activities showed little recovery benefit in parts of the evidence; the doc's "doing nothing isn't automatically recovery" note.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The contribution of various types of activities to recovery" — title

## Notes / caveats

- The findings nuance "rest = recovery": activity *content* and the person's experience matter, not just demand-absence.
- Both docs cite it honestly as *mixed evidence* for passive rest — not as a claim that all rest fails.
- **Does NOT support:** that play (voluntary, liking) recovers — Rook & Zijlstra cover leisure broadly; the play-channel evidence is Koçak/Zosh elsewhere.
