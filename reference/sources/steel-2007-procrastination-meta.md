## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- self-management claims: "The canonical account is temporal motivation theory: procrastination tracks task aversiveness, delay to reward, and low expectancy — Steel, 2007, a meta-analysis of ~691 correlations / 216 studies."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Task aversiveness is the strongest predictor** — the more unpleasant/dull/frustrating a task, the more it is postponed.
- **Delay and expectancy matter** — rewards far in the future and low self-efficacy both increase postponement; this is temporal motivation theory's core: motivation ≈ (expectancy × value) / (impulsiveness × delay).
- **Conscientiousness facets (especially self-control/distraction) correlate strongly** — procrastination is weak self-regulation, not a time-management skill deficit.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "quintessential self-regulatory failure" — title framing: procrastination is fundamentally a regulation problem, matching the shelf's emotional-regulation account.

## Notes / caveats

- Correlational meta-analysis: predictors are associations, not proven causes.
- **Does NOT support:** that procrastinators lack ability or that time-management training alone fixes it; the emotional/avoidance mechanism is the actionable layer.
