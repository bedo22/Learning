## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- rest-and-recovery claims: "Some studies find low-effort leisure does little or nothing for recovery (Rook & Zijlstra, 2006; Sonnentag & Natter, 2004 — no effect in flight attendants)."

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Leisure time did not reliably recover flight attendants** — a null finding: low-effort leisure showed little recovery effect for this demanding, irregular-shift sample.
- **The null result is sample/context-specific** — flight attendants' irregular work may undermine typical leisure recovery.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "Is there no effect during leisure?" — the title's question; the study's null-result finding

## Notes / caveats

- A null result — cited by the doc as *mixed evidence* for passive rest, honestly.
- The flight-attendant sample (irregular shifts, high strain) is unusual; effects may not generalize to office workers.
- **Does NOT support:** that leisure never recovers — it's one null study the doc balances against positive evidence.
