## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Deliberate practice explains only 12–26% of performance variance** — across domains (music, games, sports, education), practice accounts for a modest portion of the difference between individuals.
- **The Ericsson framework overstates practice's role** — while practice is necessary, it is far from sufficient. Genetic, personality, and other factors play significant roles.
- **Domain specificity matters** — the proportion of variance explained by practice varies dramatically across domains (higher in games, lower in education).

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The relationship between deliberate practice and performance was weaker than has been claimed." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a critique of Ericsson et al. (1993), not a wholesale rejection of deliberate practice.
- Ericsson et al. (2018) responded, arguing that Hambrick et al. conflated different measures of practice.
- The debate is ongoing, but the core point — practice is important but not the whole story — is now widely accepted.
- **Does NOT support:** that practice is irrelevant (it clearly matters), or that talent/genetics are the only factors (practice also matters).
