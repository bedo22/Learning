## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Bateson's framing idea — play announces itself ("this is play"), a metacommunicative frame that distinguishes play from not-play.
- play-as-recovery claims: the same action is play or not-play depending on the *frame* (Bateson) — the doc's recovery-vs-procrastination appraisal passage pairs Bateson with Reinecke & Hofmann.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Play is framed, not intrinsic to the action** — Bateson's metacommunication: the frame "this is play" makes an action play.
- **The frame is what distinguishes play from not-play** — the doc's point that the same activity can be play at 6pm and not-play at 2am traces to Bateson.
- **Play signals "this is not literal"** — the playful nip is not a bite; the doc's "the flip is named" rows use this.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "the playful nip denotes the bite, but it does not denote what would be denoted by the bite" — Bateson, "A Theory of Play and Fantasy" (the metacommunication example)
