## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **No credible evidence supports learning-styles matching** — the claim that students learn better when taught in their preferred style (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) is not supported by rigorous research.
- **The "meshing hypothesis" fails** — the idea that matching instructional method to learning style produces better outcomes has not been demonstrated in any well-designed study.
- **Learning styles inventories lack validity** — the assessments used to categorize students into "types" have poor test-retest reliability and weak predictive validity.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "There is no adequate evidence base to justify incorporating learning-styles assessments into general educational practice." — the key conclusion

> "The meshing hypothesis — that instruction should be tailored to a student's learning style — is unsupported." — on the central claim

## Notes / caveats

- This is a review paper, not a single experiment. It systematically evaluates the evidence for learning-styles theories.
- The paper does *not* claim that people don't have preferences — only that matching instruction to preferences doesn't improve learning.
- **Does NOT support:** that all learning preferences are meaningless (people do have preferences), or that multimodal instruction is useless (it often helps for other reasons).
