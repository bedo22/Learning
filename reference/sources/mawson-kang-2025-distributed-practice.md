## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Distributed practice produces a moderate benefit in classrooms** — the meta-analytic effect size was d = 0.499 (≈ 0.50), a moderate and practically meaningful effect.
- **The benefit holds across subjects and ages** — spacing worked in both STEM and non-STEM subjects, and across K-12 and university settings.
- **Longer retention intervals show larger benefits** — spacing matters most when you need to remember something for a long time, not just for tomorrow's test.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The overall effect of distributed practice on classroom learning was moderate (d = 0.499), supporting its use as an effective learning strategy." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a meta-analysis specifically of *classroom* studies, which is more applied than the typical laboratory meta-analyses.
- d = 0.50 is a moderate effect — comparable to the effect of many educational interventions.
- **Does NOT support:** that all spacing schedules are equally effective (the optimal spacing depends on the material and retention interval).
