## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: wanting (incentive salience, urge/pull) is dissociable from liking (hedonic enjoyment) — a wanting-feed (numbing, doomscrolling) can pull without producing liking.
- play-as-recovery claims: the strict list's "Liking during" test distinguishes genuine play-liking from a wanting-driven feed.
- wanting-vs-liking doc (neighbor) covers the mechanism fully.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Wanting and liking are separable reward components** — the incentive-sensitization/reward account separates dopamine-driven incentive salience (wanting) from hedonic liking.
- **Something can pull you without you enjoying it** — a wanting-feed produces approach/urge but not liking; the doc's Liking-during test exploits this gap.
- **Addiction's "incentive-sensitization" is wanting gone pathological** — 2016 extension; the doc's "numbing/wanting-feed" red-flag traces here.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "incentive salience" — wanting is "attributed by mesolimbic dopamine systems to reward-related stimuli," distinct from hedonic impact (liking) — Berridge & Robinson, 1998 abstract/model

## Notes / caveats

- The wanting/liking dissociation is strong but the *measured* dissociation uses animal psychopharmacology; doc applies the conceptual gap to human media use, which is an extension of degrees, not a direct measurement.
- The 2016 American Psychologist paper's DOI is 10.1037/amp0000059 (verified). 
- **Does NOT support:** a claim that wanting never accompanies liking (they often co-occur); the doc uses the *dissociation* (over-claimed wanting without liking = numbing), not an all-or-none split.
