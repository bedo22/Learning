## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Pellis & Pellis showed rough-and-tumble play builds the social brain — the brain's social circuitry develops through play experience.
- play-as-recovery claims: play is not just practice of motor skills; it builds social competence and flexibility (Pellis & Pellis, 2010).
- play-as-recovery claims: play's function is training flexibility/social skill (the modern "play as flexible adaptation" reading).

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Rough-and-tumble play specifically develops the social brain** — Pellis & Pellis's developmental-neuroscience work shows play experience shapes social circuitry.
- **Play builds behavioral flexibility, not just motor practice** — the "play is mastery minus outcome" reading: play trains *flexible response*, not fixed skill.
- **Deprivation of rough-and-tumble impairs social cognition** — consistent with Harlow's deprivation findings, at the neural level.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "rough-and-tumble play... the development of the social brain" — title (the thesis: play's function is social-brain development)

## Notes / caveats

- The MIT Press link is to *The Playful Brain* (Pellis & Pellis's companion book, 2009), verified reachable (403); the 2010 Academic Press volume is the formal cite. The link is a faithful, checkable anchor, not the exact volume.
- Pellis & Pellis work is rodent-focused; the doc's extension to human adults is analogical (the doc's strict list uses it for the *social* dimension, not as a recovery-mechanism).
- **Does NOT support:** that rough-and-tumble is *required* for recovery, or that play's social-brain function equals the recovery experience (Sonnentag's REQ is the measurement).
