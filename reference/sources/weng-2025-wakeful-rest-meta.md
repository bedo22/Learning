## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Wakeful rest reliably improves memory consolidation** — meta-analysis of 37 studies / 63 experiments (82 comparisons) found a significant effect, Hedges' *g* = 0.448, 95% CI [0.339, 0.557].
- **The benefit persists at least 7 days** — effect at 7 days remained reliable, *g* = 0.270, 95% CI [0.024, 0.516].
- **Older adults benefit more than younger adults** — age moderated the effect; recall (vs recognition) reflected it better.
- **The effect is robust to "how" you rest** — duration, eyes open/closed, luminance, and posture did not modulate it.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "we conducted this meta-analysis, compiled from 37 studies, including a total of 63 experiments that contributed 82 comparisons ... revealed a significant effect of wakeful rest on memory consolidation (Hedges's g = 0.448 ...), and this effect persists even after 7 days (Hedges's g = 0.270 ...)" — abstract

## Notes / caveats

- This is the aggregate quantitative support behind the doc's "10–15 min of quiet post-study is a cheap consolidation booster."
- Wakeful-rest consolidation is compared against active wakefulness; the doc is careful not to equate it with sleep consolidation.
- **Does NOT support:** that wakeful rest replaces sleep — the largest effect is still the sleep literature the doc cites separately (Diekelmann & Born 2010).
