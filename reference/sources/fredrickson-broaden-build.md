## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: positive emotions (like play's joy) broaden thought-action repertoires and build personal resources over time (broaden-and-build).
- play-as-recovery cites Fredrickson 1998/2004 as the theory under the "recovery restores flexibility / positive affect" dimension.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Positive emotions broaden attention and thought-action repertoires** — supported by the 1998 model's core claim (opposite of negative emotions' narrowing).
- **Repeated positive emotions build durable resources** ("build") — the 2004 review consolidates the broaden-and-build formulation.
- **This is why play feels restorative, not just pleasurable** — play's positive affect broadens; the doc uses this under recovery (flexibility, positive affect).

## Key quotes (with locations)

> broaden-and-build (2004): "certain discrete positive emotions — including joy, interest, contentment... — broaden people's momentary thought-action repertoires and build their enduring personal resources"

## Notes / caveats

- The broadened thought-action *repertoire* (2004) is a theoretical claim, not a single decisive experiment; it is a widely-cited but synthetic framework.
- The doc uses it as the theory *under* the positivity/flexibility recovery dimension; the empirical *measurement* of recovery comes from Sonnentag elsewhere.
- **Does NOT support:** that any positive emotion is *play* (needs the definitional tests), or that broaden-and-build alone produces recovery-detachment; it's one component.
