## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **The longhand advantage is small-to-moderate** — across studies, longhand produces a small but reliable advantage over laptop for conceptual learning.
- **The effect shrinks when distraction is controlled** — when studies control for the distraction of having a laptop (e.g., internet access), the longhand advantage diminishes.
- **Format matters** — the advantage is larger for free-response tests than for multiple-choice tests.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "The overall effect favoring longhand was small (d = 0.25), and diminished when distraction was controlled." — the key finding

## Notes / caveats

- This is a meta-analysis synthesizing multiple studies, including both Urry et al. (2021) and the original Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014).
- d = 0.25 is a small effect — real but not dramatic.
- **Does NOT support:** that longhand is always better (the effect is small and context-dependent), or that laptops should be banned (they can be beneficial when used well).
