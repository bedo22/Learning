## What the doc(s) claim from this source

- play-as-recovery claims: Gray's longitudinal analyses show a modern decline of free play (children's free play dropped across decades) — the doc's cultural-context note.
- play-as-recovery claims: free play is the *default* developmental context for learning self-control and social skill (Gray, 2013).
- play-as-recovery claims: "the cultural context matters" — play's absence is a modern, reversible condition.

## Key findings (one line each, with supporting quote)

- **Free play has declined sharply over decades** — Gray's analyses document the drop in children's unstructured play across the 20th/21st century.
- **Declining free play tracks rising anxiety/depression** — Gray argues the two trends are linked; the doc's cultural-context note uses this.
- **Play is the evolutionary-default way children learn** — Gray's evolutionary lens: children learn through self-directed play, not adult instruction.

## Key quotes (with locations)

> "free play" decline — Gray's trend analyses (play declined; anxiety/depression rose; argued causal link)

## Notes / caveats

- Gray's claims are argued from trend correlations + evolutionary theory, not RCTs; the anxiety linkage is correlational and debated. The doc uses it as *cultural context*, not mechanism.
- The Psychology Today blog link is Gray's ongoing public column (verified reachable); the book is the formal cite but has no DOI.
- **Does NOT support:** that adult recovery requires Gray's thesis, or that declining play *causes* adult recovery deficits; it's the cultural backdrop.
