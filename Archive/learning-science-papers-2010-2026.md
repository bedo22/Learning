# Learning-Science Papers 2010–2026 — a year-by-year survey for the Learn to Learn shelf

Purpose: identify important, verifiable science papers (2010–2026) that can strengthen the
reference shelf (`reference/*.html`) and the "learning to learn" topic as a whole. Every entry
below was checked against its primary source (journal page, publisher record, or DOI) during
research. Entries already cited inside the shelf docs are flagged as *already cited* so the
reader knows where the shelf stands today.

Papers are grouped by **publication year** (some famous papers are routinely miscited one year
late — e.g. Karpicke & Blunt is 2011, not 2010). Each entry ends with `improves →` tags naming
the shelf doc(s) it most directly upgrades.

Doc key: **study** = study-advice · **orders** = orders-of-learning · **memory** =
memory-techniques · **system** = learning-system · **myths** = learning-myths · **skill** =
skill-acquisition · **productivity** = productivity-systems · **self** = self-management ·
**rest** = rest-and-recovery · **play** = play-as-recovery · **goals** = reverse-goal-setting

## 2010

- **Diekelmann, S., & Born, J. (2010). The memory function of sleep. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 114–126.** https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2762
  The canonical modern review of sleep-dependent memory consolidation (system consolidation, replay, slow-wave/REM division of labour).
  `improves → memory, rest, system`

- **Killingsworth, M. A., & Gilbert, D. T. (2010). A wandering mind is an unhappy mind. *Science*, 330(6006), 932.** *(already cited in play)*

- **Hsee, C. K., Yang, A. X., & Wang, L. (2010). Idleness aversion and the need for justifiable busyness. *Psychological Science*, 21(7), 926–930.** *(already cited in play)*

## 2011

- **Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011). Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping. *Science*, 331(6018), 772–775.** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327
  The landmark showing retrieval practice beats elaborative studying (concept mapping) on long-term, high-level learning — not just memory. The shelf's core "encoding vs retrieval" claim rests on this.
  `improves → orders, system, study`

- **McDaniel, M. A., Agarwal, P. K., Huelser, B. J., McDermott, K. B., & Roediger, H. L. (2011). Test-enhanced learning in a middle school science classroom: The effects of quiz frequency and placement. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*, 17(1), 21–33.**
  The testing effect moving out of the lab and into a real classroom; frequent, low-stakes quizzing beats re-reading. Bridges the memory science to daily study practice.
  `improves → study`

## 2012

- **Rohrer, D., & Pashler, H. (2012). Learning styles: Where's the evidence? *Medical Education*, 46(7), 634–635.** https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2923.2012.04273.x
  The decisive "where's the evidence?" short paper on the learning-styles neuromyth — almost no studies support matching instruction to style.
  `improves → myths`

- **Karpicke, J. D. (2012). Retrieval-based learning: Active retrieval promotes meaningful learning. *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 21(3), 157–163.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721412443552
  Framework paper: retrieval as a *learning event* in itself (not a test of learning). Feeds directly into how the shelf explains retrieval practice.
  `improves → study, memory`

- **Carpenter, S. K., Cepeda, N. J., Rohrer, D., Kang, S. H. K., & Pashler, H. (2012). Using spacing to enhance diverse forms of learning: Review of recent research and implications for instruction. *Educational Psychology Review*, 24(3), 369–378.**
  Spacing benefits far more than rote memory — inductive, creative, and skill learning too. The "spacing works for everything" citation the shelf can lean on.
  `improves → study, orders`

## 2013

- **Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques: Promising directions from cognitive and educational psychology. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 4–58.** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
  The ten-techniques mega-review (practice testing and distributed practice top the utility rankings; rereading, highlighting, and summarization bottom out). The single most citable anchor for the whole shelf.
  `improves → study, orders, myths`

- **Melby-Lervåg, M., & Hulme, C. (2013). Is working memory training effective? A meta-analytic review. *Developmental Psychology*, 49(2), 270–291.** https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028228
  Working-memory "brain training" shows near-transfer gains only; far transfer to intelligence/reading/math is weak or absent. Direct evidence for a learning myth the shelf should pre-empt.
  `improves → myths, memory`

- **Sana, F., Weston, T., & Cepeda, N. J. (2013). Laptop multitasking hinders classroom learning for both users and nearby peers. *Computers & Education*, 62, 24–31.**
  Multitasking on a laptop harms the user *and* the students who can see the screen. Core evidence for the distraction sections.
  `improves → productivity, self, study`

## 2014

- **Mueller, P. A., & Oppenheimer, D. M. (2014). The pen is mightier than the keyboard: Advantages of longhand over laptop note taking. *Psychological Science*, 25(6), 1159–1168.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614524581
  Longhand note-taking beats verbatim laptop notes on conceptual learning; verbatim capture is the mechanism that hurts. (Later replications nuance it — worth citing with the caveat.)
  `improves → study`

- **Macnamara, B. N., Hambrick, D. Z., & Oswald, F. L. (2014). Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, professions, and education: A meta-analysis. *Psychological Science*, 25(8), 1608–1618.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810
  The famous ~12%-of-variance estimate: deliberate practice explains only a modest share of performance — the evidence against pure 10,000-hours. Critical nuance for the skill-acquisition doc (which currently leans on deliberate practice).
  `improves → skill`

- **Rowland, C. A. (2014). The effect of testing versus restudy on retention: A meta-analytic review of the testing effect. *Psychological Bulletin*, 140(6), 1432–1463.** https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559
  Meta-analysis confirming testing beats restudy robustly; also shows people's *metacognitive* belief that restudy works is the barrier — desirable-difficulty framing.
  `improves → study, orders`

- **Gruber, M. J., Gelman, B. D., & Ranganath, C. (2014). States of curiosity modulate hippocampus-dependent learning via the dopaminergic circuit. *Neuron*, 84(2), 486–496.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.08.060
  Curiosity states boost hippocampus-dependent learning, and even learning of *incidental* material presented while curious. Scientific underpinning for "curiosity is a learning lever" and for the shelf's motivation angle.
  `improves → system, memory`

## 2015

- **Rohrer, D., Dedrick, R. F., & Stershic, S. (2015). Interleaved practice improves mathematics learning. *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 107(3), 900–908.**
  A real-classroom, weeks-long experiment: interleaved math practice beats blocked practice — and students *believe* blocked worked better (metacognitive illusion). Best concrete interleaving citation.
  `improves → study, orders`

## 2016

- **Kang, S. H. K. (2016). Spaced repetition promotes efficient and effective learning: Policy implications for instruction. *Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 3(1), 12–19.** https://doi.org/10.1177/2372732215624708
  Short, citable policy review of spaced repetition — including the expanding-schedule evidence and why cramming fails retention.
  `improves → study, orders`

- **Simons, D. J., et al. (2016). Do "brain-training" programs work? *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 17(3), 103–186.** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100616661983
  The definitive multi-author verdict on brain-training claims (Lumosity et al.): no compelling evidence of far transfer. Strongest possible citation for the "brain training is a myth" section.
  `improves → myths`

- **Reinecke, L., & Hofmann, W. (2016). Slacking off or winding down? ... *(already cited in play)*; Hunter, E. M., & Wu, C. (2016). Give me a better break ... *(already cited in play)***

## 2017

- **Adesope, O. O., Trevisan, D. A., & Sundararajan, N. (2017). Rethinking the use of tests: A meta-analysis of practice testing. *Review of Educational Research*, 87(3), 659–701.** https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306
  Large meta (217 studies) of practice testing, including moderators (feedback, delay, format). Strongest single "testing works, here's the boundary conditions" citation.
  `improves → study, orders`

- **Metcalfe, J. (2017). Learning from errors. *Annual Review of Psychology*, 68, 465–489.** https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044022
  Errors — even committed with high confidence and corrected — produce better retention than errorless study, given corrective feedback. Evidence for "fail loudly, then correct" as a learning strategy.
  `improves → study, skill`

- **Ward, A. F., Duke, K., Gneezy, A., & Bos, M. W. (2017). Brain drain: The mere presence of one's own smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity. *Journal of the Association for Consumer Research*, 2(2), 140–154.** *(already cited in play)*

## 2018

- **Sisk, V. F., Burgoyne, A. P., Sun, J., Butler, J. L., & Macnamara, B. N. (2018). To what extent and under which circumstances are growth mind-sets important to academic achievement? Two meta-analyses. *Psychological Science*, 29(4), 549–571.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704
  Mindset–achievement correlation is weak and intervention effects are small — the honest counterweight to pop "growth mindset" hype. The shelf's myths doc should carry this.
  `improves → myths, goals`

## 2019

- **Yeager, D. S., et al. (2019). A national experiment reveals where a growth mindset improves achievement. *Nature*, 573(7774), 364–369.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y
  The preregistered national experiment (N ≈ 12,000): a brief mindset intervention helped *lower-achieving* students (and in a supportive context) — the nuanced, conditions-based version of the mindset story that belongs next to Sisk et al.
  `improves → myths, goals`

## 2020

- **Kuhfeld, M., Soland, J., Tarasawa, B., Johnson, A., Ruzek, E., & Liu, J. (2020). Projecting the potential impact of COVID-19 school closures on academic achievement. *Educational Researcher*, 49(8), 549–565.** https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X20965918
  The initial projection model for pandemic learning loss (later confirmed by real data) — context for why recovery, rest, and spaced resumption matter.
  `improves → study, system`

- **Tomova, L., et al. (2020). Acute social isolation evokes midbrain craving responses similar to hunger. *Nature Neuroscience*, 23(12), 1597–1605.** *(already cited in play)*

## 2021

- **Agarwal, P. K., Nunes, L. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2021). Retrieval practice consistently benefits student learning: A systematic review of applied research in schools and classrooms. *Educational Psychology Review*, 33(4), 1409–1453.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9
  The classroom-side companion to Adesope: retrieval practice benefits learning in real schools across grades and subjects, with the design conditions (feedback, spacing) that make it work.
  `improves → study`

- **Sunday, O. J., Adesope, O. O., & Maarhuis, P. L. (2021). The effects of smartphone addiction on learning: A meta-analysis. *Computers in Human Behavior Reports*, 4, 100114.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2021.100114
  Meta-analysis (44 studies): smartphone addiction negatively associates with learning and academic performance (r ≈ −0.12). The quantitative spine for the distraction sections. *(Correlational — present as association, not causation.)*
  `improves → productivity, self`

- **Steed, L. B., Swider, B. W., Keem, S., & Liu, J. T. (2021). Leaving work at work: A meta-analysis on employee recovery from work. *Journal of Management*, 47(4).** *(already cited in play)*

## 2022

- **Sonnentag, S., Zhao, C., & Parker, S. L. (2022). Recovery from work: Advancing the field toward the future. *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior*, 9, 33–60.** *(already cited in play)*
- **Milestone (not a paper): ChatGPT released, November 2022.** The start of the generative-AI era that re-opens the "learning vs performance" question (see 2024–2025).

## 2023

- *Light year for the shelf.* The experimental evidence on generative AI and learning arrived mostly in 2024–2025 (below). A useful 2023 pointer: the first wave of "AI in education" reviews and the Stanford/SIEPR debates on AI and human capital. No single landmark paper worth adding to the shelf yet.

## 2024

- **Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabaci, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2024). Generative AI can harm learning. *NBER Working Paper No. 31161*.** (Published 2025 in *PNAS*, see below.)
  RCT in high-school math (Turkey): using an unconstrained GPT gave students a *performance* boost during practice while *reducing* actual learning on the real exam — and students were unaware. The cleanest demonstration that the shelf's retrieval/effort principle extends into the AI era: an answer machine is a crutch that masquerades as learning.
  `improves → study, system, play (meaning-trap section), all AI-era sections`

- **Koçak, Ö. E., Gorgievski, M. J., & Bakker, A. B. (2024). Recovery from work by playing video games. *Applied Psychology*, 73(4).** *(already cited in play)*
- **Headrick, L., et al. (2024). ... recovery experiences mega-analysis. *Journal of Business and Psychology*.** *(already cited in play)*

## 2025

- **Kestin, G., et al. (2025). AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: An RCT... *Scientific Reports*, 15.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6
  Large Harvard physics-course RCT: a Socratic, retrieval-prompting AI tutor produced ~double the learning gains in *less* time than in-class active learning, with higher engagement. The guardrail side of the 2024 finding: AI that *prompts* effort helps; AI that *replaces* it harms.
  `improves → study, system` (and a perfect paired citation with Bastani)

- **Bastani, H., et al. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *PNAS*, 122(24), e2422633122.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
  Peer-reviewed version of the 2024 NBER paper; adds the key moderator: a "guardrailed" tutor that withholds answers *did* improve real learning (practice +0.361 vs base +0.137 on immediate, but only the guardrailed version translated to the real exam).
  `improves → study, system`

- **Gerlich, M. (2025). AI tools in society: Impacts on cognitive offloading and the future of critical thinking. *Societies*, 15(1), 6.** https://doi.org/10.3390/soc15010006
  Correlational survey (N = 666): higher AI-tool use/dependence associates with more cognitive offloading and lower critical-thinking scores; younger users most affected. *Correlational — use as hypothesis, not proof.*
  `improves → system, productivity, self`

- **Weng, L., et al. (2025). Effects of wakeful rest on memory consolidation: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*.** https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-025-02665-x
  Meta-analytic support for the classic Dewar finding: brief *wakeful rest* after learning improves memory vs. active distraction. This is the missing evidence for the rest-and-recovery doc's "quiet rest consolidates" claim — and it pairs beautifully with the play doc's incubation section.
  `improves → rest, memory, system`

- **Zhao, et al. (2025). Work hard, play harder. *CHI 2025*. *(already cited in play)*; Costantini, et al. (2025). Playful work design. *J. Business & Psychology*. *(already cited in play)***

## 2026

- **Lutz, N. D., et al. (2026). Sleep's contribution to memory formation. *Physiological Reviews*.** https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00054.2024
  The current authoritative physiology review (replacing the 2010/2013 classics) of how sleep consolidates, reorganizes, and protects learned material — the 2026 update for the rest/memory docs.
  `improves → memory, rest, system`

- **Proyer, R. T., & Sendatzki, S. (2026). Examining play and playfulness at work. *Annual Review of Organizational Psychology and Organizational Behavior*.** *(already cited in play)*

- **Stanford SCALE (2026). Understanding the evidence base on AI in K-12 education** (report analyzing 800+ papers as of Oct 2025). Useful survey pointer for the AI-era sections rather than a single study.
  `improves → study, system`

## Priority shortlist — what to add to the docs first

Ranked by (evidence strength × how much the current shelf is missing it):

1. **Weng et al. 2025 (wakeful rest meta)** → rest-and-recovery: adds the "quiet rest consolidates memory" evidence the doc currently implies but doesn't cite.
2. **Bastani et al. 2024/2025 + Kestin et al. 2025 (paired)** → study-advice / learning-system: a new "AI and learning" subsection — the technology that *replaces* retrieval harms (Bastani), the one that *prompts* it helps (Kestin). Directly extends the doc's effort principle into the current era.
3. **Dunlosky et al. 2013** → study-advice / orders-of-learning: the ten-techniques utility ranking as a summary table.
4. **Macnamara et al. 2014** → skill-acquisition: deliberate practice explains ~12% of variance — necessary nuance against the 10,000-hour framing.
5. **Rohrer & Pashler 2012 + Simons et al. 2016** → learning-myths: learning styles and brain training, two neuromyths with definitive citations.
6. **Sisk et al. 2018 + Yeager et al. 2019 (paired)** → learning-myths: the honest, conditions-based mindset story.
7. **Rowland 2014 + Adesope 2017 + Agarwal 2021** → study-advice: the testing-effect evidence ladder (lab meta → classroom meta → applied systematic review).
8. **Gruber et al. 2014** → learning-system: curiosity as a dopamine-driven memory amplifier.
9. **Sunday et al. 2021 + Sana et al. 2013** → productivity-systems / self-management: quantified smartphone-learning association and the laptop-multitasking spillover effect.
10. **Diekelmann & Born 2010 (+ Lutz 2026 as the update)** → memory-techniques / rest: the sleep-consolidation backbone.

## Notes on method

- Every paper above was verified against a primary source (journal page, PubMed/publisher record, or DOI) via web search in August 2026; citations reflect the verified publication venue and year.
- "Already cited in play" flags papers the play-as-recovery doc already carries — listed here only for completeness of the timeline.
- Watch the caveats: Gerlich (2025) and Sunday (2021) are correlational; Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014) has been partially replicated with nuance; Bastani's harm finding is specifically about *unguarded* answer-providing AI.
