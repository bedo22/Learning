## 2. What's strong

1. **The retrieval/encoding spine is coherent and now evidence-backed.** Karpicke & Blunt (2011), Dunlosky (2013), Rowland (2014), Adesope (2017), Agarwal (2021) anchor the "test yourself" message across study-advice, learning-system, orders, memory.
2. **Recovery docs are the deepest.** rest-and-recovery and play-as-recovery together form a genuinely sophisticated treatment (effort-recovery, four experiences, wanting/liking, recovery triage, stop-signal) with honest counterargument sections — rare for this genre.
3. **The 2021–2026 evidence pass (this session) closed the biggest citation gaps**: study-advice (zero research cites → full ladder), learning-myths, skill-acquisition, learning-system, productivity, orders, rest (wakeful rest).
4. **AI-era content now exists** (study-advice "AI and learning" with the paired Bastani/Kestin RCTs) and is cross-linked to the play doc's wanting/liking frame.
5. **Cross-linking is unusually good** — the shelf reads as one system (time → focus → energy → play → encoding → retrieval).

## 3. Content audit — doc by doc

### 01 · blooms-six-levels-of-thinking — *weakest evidence*
- **Gaps:** essentially no research citations beyond the taxonomy itself; the "misinterpreted effort hypothesis" (a key concept, referenced by learning-myths) is asserted, not sourced; no classroom evidence that assessing at higher levels improves transfer.
- **Improve:** cite the effort/retrieval evidence (Bjork desirable difficulties; Rowland 2014), add a SOLO-taxonomy parallel, and — timely — add a short "use Bloom's to interrogate AI answers" note (evaluate AI output at each level), matching the new AI section.

### 02 · learning-myths
- **Gaps:** covers 5 myths (time/IQ, learning styles, easy-learning, brain training, mindset) but misses the classic pop-science myths that still circulate: "we use 10% of the brain", "left-brain/right-brain", "multitasking is efficient", "sleep is wasted time", "memory is like a recording".
- **Improve:** add 2–3 of these with one-line debunks (neuromyth surveys; Sana et al. 2013 for multitasking; Diekelmann & Born 2010 for sleep). Also fix the title line "Over 95% of people hold at least one" — it now refers to five myths, not three (minor copy fix).

### 03 · learning-system
- **Gaps:** the system stops at "encoding + retrieval" — the **consolidation** leg (sleep, wakeful rest) is only cross-linked, not owned; curiosity is now a callout (good); no explicit treatment of interleaving or the desirable-difficulties family.
- **Improve:** add a "the third leg: consolidation" subsection (wakeful rest meta, sleep), or move consolidation to a dedicated Sleep doc and link both ways. Add interleaving as an encoding tactic with the Rohrer 2015 evidence.

### 04 · orders-of-learning — *smallest doc*
- **Gaps:** the "higher vs lower order" idea deserves the desirable-difficulties framework (Bjork): why harder-at-first = better-retained, and the metacognitive illusion (students *believe* blocked/easy study works — Rohrer 2015, Rowland 2014 both show this).
- **Improve:** add a "why higher order feels worse but works" subsection with those two citations; consider whether it should fold into learning-system (it currently carries real distinct content — keep, but enrich).

### 05 · self-management
- **Gaps:** distraction is treated as short-term fixes vs root cause, but the *quantified* evidence (Sana 2013, Sunday 2021) is only in the cite block now, not the body; attention-residue (Leroy 2009 — switching costs when tasks are unfinished) is absent; decision-fatigue ↔ PFC-fatigue tie is implied but never stated.
- **Improve:** add an attention-residue callout (the "shutdown ritual" in rest-and-recovery is its practical fix — cross-link both ways), and move one sentence of the distraction stats into the focus section.

### 06 · memory-techniques — *evidence-dense, one blind spot*
- **Gaps:** for "the memory doc" there is **no sleep-consolidation section**; the doc stops at handling/retrieval. (Deliberate scope, but a reader looking for "how do I make it stick" finds no sleep advice.) Length is already high.
- **Improve:** add a short "sleep is the second half of memory" callout (Diekelmann & Born 2010; Lutz 2026) cross-linking rest-and-recovery — or leave it to a new Sleep doc and link. Either way, link.

### 07 · study-advice — *now the best-rounded*
- **Gaps:** no exam-week/test-day strategy (test anxiety, performance under pressure); no dedicated note-taking lesson (scattered across memory/skill docs); spacing *schedule* template is mentioned but not shown (the 2/7/30 rhythm lives only inside the forgetting-curve doc and the play doc's rest menu).
- **Improve:** add a "the week before the exam" mini-section; a note-taking lesson consolidating longhand-vs-laptop + nonlinear mapping + cognitive offload; a concrete spacing schedule example.

### 08 · rest-and-recovery — *rich, approaching oversized*
- **Gaps:** sleep is treated as "a distinct biological process" but never given its own section (architecture, debt, naps, sleep vs recovery); burnout warning signs are named in the caution but not developed.
- **Improve:** either add a compact sleep subsection (with Lutz 2026 / Diekelmann & Born 2010 — now only in the sources) or spin a Sleep & Learning doc; add a 3-line burnout checklist. Watch length (34 KB).

### 09 · productivity-systems
- **Gaps:** the weekly ritual is the heart but has no *evidence* that weekly planning beats daily reacting (it's asserted as practice); time-blocking and energy-management research are absent.
- **Improve:** cite time-blocking / weekly-review evidence lightly; add an "energy ≠ willpower" tie to self-management and rest. Small doc, small gap — low priority.

### 10 · reverse-goal-setting — *the doc most needing content*
- **Gaps:** the 5-step method is entirely uncited (2 mentions of "cite" in the file). Goal-setting is one of the most researched topics in psychology: **Locke & Latham** (goal specificity/difficulty), **WOOP** (Oettingen — mental contrasting + implementation intentions), **self-concordance** (Sheldon & Elliot — goals aligned with identity), **TOTE/implementation intentions** (already in productivity).
- **Improve:** this is the single highest-value content upgrade available: add a "what the goal-setting science says" section (Locke & Latham 2002 review; Oettingen 2012; Sheldon & Elliot 1999), and tie the future-self step to the identity/self-concordance research. The method is good; it deserves its evidence.

### 11 · skill-acquisition
- **Gaps:** feedback is central to RAIL (Awareness stage) but the feedback *science* is uncited (Hattie & Timperley 2007 — "feedback is among the most powerful influences on achievement, but its effects vary enormously by type"); the 10,000-hours myth is only implicitly addressed; transfer of training untouched.
- **Improve:** add a feedback callout (Hattie & Timperley; the "what to do about it" direction of feedback), and one line on transfer (practice in varied contexts — Rohrer's interleaving).

### 12 · play-as-recovery — *oversized (85 KB EN, 113 KB AR)*
- **Gaps:** none major in content — the problem is *shape*. It duplicates the recovery triage from rest-and-recovery (intentional, but should link instead of restate), carries a long Islamic-fiqh section (valuable, but appendix material for most readers), and an enormous flat reference list.
- **Improve:** per the existing `Archive/Play as recovery Review.txt`: split into a core page + appendices (fiqh details, full reference list); compress the reference block into categorized bullets; dedupe the triage by linking to rest-and-recovery's. Also strip the "1 · / 2 ·" numerals from h3 headings (violates the shelf's no-numerals rule — see §6).

### Glossary (80 terms)
- **Missing terms** the new content introduced: *eudaimonic entertainment*, *wakeful rest*, *cognitive offloading* (verify), *fluency illusion*, *zero-outcome block* (verify), *wanting/liking* (verify), plus AI-era terms (*prompting/guardrails*, *AI fluency illusion*).
- **Improve:** run a term-audit against the docs (every `<strong>` that reads as a term should be a glossary entry), then add ~6–10 entries.

## 4. Missing topics — the biggest content gaps

1. **Motivation & Self-Determination** (highest value). Deci & Ryan's SDT (autonomy/competence/relatedness), intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation, motivation crowding, why learners quit. Currently scattered one-liners (play doc, rest doc affiliation callout). A dedicated doc would anchor "why do I stick with this?" — the meta-layer above every technique.
2. **Metacognition & Calibration.** Judgments of learning, the illusion of competence ("feeling easy ≠ learning"), knowing-what-you-don't-know, self-monitoring. This is the *scientific name* for the shelf's most repeated lesson (test yourself, avoid the illusion) — it deserves its own doc.
3. **Sleep & Learning.** The consolidation literature (Diekelmann & Born 2010; Lutz 2026; wakeful-rest meta) is now scattered across rest/memory/learning-system. A dedicated Sleep doc (or major section) would own it.
4. **AI & Learning** (promote from subsection). Bastani/Kestin/Gerlich + LLM-metacognition work (2025–2026) is growing fast enough to warrant its own reference doc: how to use AI as an interrogator, when it harms, cognitive-offloading risks.
5. **Note-taking** (consolidate). Longhand-vs-laptop evidence, nonlinear mapping, cognitive offload, "write less" — currently spread over memory-techniques, skill-acquisition, study-advice.
6. **Exam & Test Strategy.** Test anxiety, performance under pressure, exam-week protocol, mistake-triage on real exams.
7. **Attention & Distraction** (optional). Media multitasking, attention residue, deep work, environment design — partially in self-management; only worth splitting out if self-management grows.

## 6. Structural & format inconsistencies

1. **Two HTML house styles.** Old docs: `<!DOCTYPE html>` (uppercase), `<meta charset="utf-8">` (no self-close), 2-space indentation. play-as-recovery + its AR twin: `<!doctype html>`, self-closing `<meta … />`, 6-space body indent. Harmless to render, confusing to edit — pick one and convert.
2. **Heading numerals.** The house rule (reference-doc skill) is "headings carry no numerals" — but play-as-recovery's h3s use "1 ·", "2 ·", "Pillar 1 ·"… and the AR twin mirrors them. Strip for consistency (or formally allow numerals in the convention).
3. **Missing anchor ids.** Only rest-and-recovery has `id="key-takeaways"`. Cross-doc section links (play → rest#key-takeaways works; most others can't link at section level). Add `id="sec-…"` to every h2 (the house template specifies this and it's mostly not followed).
4. **Kicker numbering** is consistent (01–12 + unnumbered glossary) — good.
5. **AR twins: 2 of 13.** Only play and rest have Arabic twins. The glossary is the terminology gate for the translate skill and has no twin — translate it first, then the fundamentals (study-advice, memory-techniques, learning-system).

## 7. Lessons & learning-records

- **lessons/** (23 pages) is a genuinely good practice layer (0001 studying≠learning … 0018 RAIL), but several re-cover reference content (0005 recovery break ↔ rest; 0007b–f play sub-lessons ↔ play; 0014/0015 ↔ memory). Intentional, but there's no **lesson → reference index**; a reader can't tell which lesson deepens which doc. Add a mapping table (e.g., in `NOTES.md` or a new `lessons/README.md`).
- **learning-records/** has only 2 entries — and study-advice lesson 16 argues the learning log is one of the highest-leverage habits. Encourage maintenance as part of each future session.

## 8. Process & tooling gaps

1. **Conventions live in agent skills, not the repo.** The shelf template (reference-doc skill) and the Arabic-sync discipline (translate-to-arabic skill) are in `~/.agents/skills/`, invisible to anyone (human or agent) opening the repo. Commit a repo-level `AGENTS.md` (or `reference/CONVENTIONS.md`) capturing: spine order, no-numerals rule, id anchors, citation format, AR twin rules.
2. **The translate-to-arabic skill references pipeline scripts** (`twin-pipeline.py`, `verify-twins.py`, `splice-sections.py`) and a `maps/<doc>.json` + `PROGRESS.md` that **don't exist in this environment** — the recent AR sync was done by hand. Either vendor those scripts into the repo (e.g., `reference/scripts/`) or codify the manual checks; without this, AR sync quality depends on whoever runs it.
3. **Housekeeping:** three files carry pre-existing uncommitted edits (`Archive/Play as recovery Review.txt`, `Archive/Rest and Recovery Review.txt`, `Trascriptions/Productivity is Hard Until You Build Systems Like This.txt`). Commit or revert them so `git status` is clean.
