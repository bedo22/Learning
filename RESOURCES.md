## Underlying research / frameworks

- **Bloom's revised taxonomy** — Anderson, L. W., & Krathwohl, D. R. (Eds.) (2001). *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing* (a revision of Bloom, 1956). Readable entry point: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_taxonomy — *framework* — The six-level cognitive hierarchy Justin Sung adapts. Built for educators/exam writers; most curricula are designed around it.
- **Desirable difficulties** — Robert Bjork (UCLA). *to verify* — Related to the "misinterpreted effort hypothesis": effortful learning builds storage strength. Leads to confirm in a later session.
- **Ebbinghaus forgetting curve** (1885) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgetting_curve — *research* — The decay-of-memory curve; basis for spaced retrieval.
- **Spaced retrieval / spaced repetition** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_repetition — *research* — The frequency lever for flattening decay.
- **Schema theory** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schema_(psychology) — *research* — Why networked/connected knowledge is remembered better (higher-order learning).
- **Learning-styles myth (debunk)** — Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer &amp; Bjork (2008), "Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_styles#Criticism — *research* — The review often cited as the conclusive debunk.
- **Zeigarnik effect** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeigarnik_effect — *research* — The brain's drive to finish started tasks; basis for the "just start" tactic.
- **Eisenhower matrix** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_management#The_Eisenhower_Method — *tool* — Urgent/important prioritization.
- **Working memory capacity (Miller's law)** — Miller (1956), "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two"; Cowan (2001) ~4 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_memory — *research* — The 5–9 item weight limit of working memory.
- **Priming (psychology)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priming_(psychology) — *research* — Scoping a subject before learning it; high correlation with learning outcomes.
- **Method of loci (memory palace)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_loci — *technique* — The ancient mnemonic for rote memorization.
- **Declarative vs procedural memory** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declarative_memory — *research* — The "what" vs "how" of knowledge; most subjects need both.
- **Work recovery (Sonnentag &amp; Fritz)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_recovery — *research* — The four recovery experiences: relaxation, psychological detachment, mastery, control.
- **Attention Restoration Theory / soft fascination (Kaplan)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_restoration_theory — *research* — Why nature restores attention, energy, and working memory.
- **Implementation intentions (scripted actions)** — Gollwitzer (1999) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implementation_intention — *research* — "If-then" plans ~double goal attainment by bypassing in-the-moment decisions.
- **Habit / cue–response** — Wood &amp; Neal — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habit — *research* — Habits form from clear cue→response pairings and run outside the prefrontal cortex.
- **Force field analysis (Lewin)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force-field_analysis — *tool* — Examining driving &amp; restraining forces around a change; step 4 of reverse goal setting.
- **Process vs outcome goals** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_setting — *research* — Outcomes are symptoms of the process you control; the meta-goal is becoming the person for whom the outcome is easy.
- **Four stages of competence** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_stages_of_competence — *framework* — The classic model RAIL is derived from.
- **Latent learning (Tolman)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_learning — *research* — Learning without immediate reinforcement; why complex skills are hard.
- **Skill decay / skill fade** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skill_fade — *research* — Neglected skills deteriorate back to earlier stages.
